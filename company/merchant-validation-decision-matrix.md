## The default threshold convention — stated once, applied throughout

Most rows below use the same pattern-recurrence rule rather than a bespoke number each time, so it's stated once here instead of repeated:

> **Default threshold: a specific answer, name, or pattern recurring independently across 3 or more of the first 10 respondents (≈30%) is logged as a candidate signal** — not acted on unilaterally, but written into the matching artifact (see each row) as a named candidate. **The same pattern recurring in 3+ of a subsequent batch of 10 (or holding at a proportionally consistent share as the sample grows) reinforces it from "candidate" toward an actual decision or backlog action.**

This mirrors two things already established elsewhere in this project, rather than inventing a new standard: the Product Owner's own explicit example for Q15 ("if many merchants independently identify the same improvement, that's an extremely strong Product signal" — operationalized below as exactly 3+ of the first 10); and `merchant-validation-campaign.md` Deliverable 5's general confidence-threshold discipline that nothing at this sample size is proof, full stop.

**A note on precision, corrected 2026-08-15 (sixth pass, M3).** Earlier drafts of this document justified the default convention above by citing Deliverable 5's "n=20 carries roughly ±20pp margin of error" figure — but that figure applies at n=20, not at n=10, where every row below actually fires by default. The real worst-case 95% margin at n=10 is closer to **±31pp**, not ±20pp. For the great majority of rows below, this imprecision has low practical consequence: the default convention only ever produces a **non-binding "candidate signal,"** logged for tracking, never a unilateral decision or escalation — the exact statistical margin doesn't change what action a candidate signal triggers (none, beyond being written down and watched). Precise statistical language is reserved for the small number of rows where a threshold triggers something consequential on its own — **Q5's Task 3/7 completion-gap rule** and **Q13's free-tier-adoption escalation**, both corrected below (Section B and Section D respectively) to state the real n=10 margin explicitly and to gate their stronger claims at the sample size where ±20pp actually applies.

A handful of rows need a different threshold shape because the question itself is structured differently (a scale, a forced binary opt-in, a context variable rather than a trigger) — those state their own reasoning explicitly rather than forcing the default convention where it doesn't fit.

## What this document deliberately does not do

It doesn't restate the questions themselves, the branching copy, or the closing-text variants — those live in `merchant-validation-campaign.md` Deliverable 3/4, and duplicating them here would create exactly the drift risk `company/CLAUDE.md`'s "reference, don't duplicate" convention exists to prevent. It doesn't set a go/no-go bar for any hypothesis — every threshold above triggers *logging a candidate signal or escalating a question*, never a unilateral resolution; Deliverable 5's Confidence thresholds section is the standing reminder that nothing at this sample size is proof. It doesn't invent new Decision Ownership categories — every routing column above uses the same three-way Architect/Product/Business classification `company/CLAUDE.md` already defines, applied to this specific instrument rather than redefined for it.
