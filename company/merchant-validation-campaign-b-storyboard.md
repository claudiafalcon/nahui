# Campaign B (Venta rápida) — Storyboard & Motion Plan, v2 (creative redesign)

Storyboard, shot list, and motion/overlay plan for Campaign B, redesigned against the **2026-08-20 creative redesign pass** of `company/merchant-validation-campaign-videos.md` (Campaign B, §B.1, including the `brand-guardian` remediation block and its same-day addendum inside Beat 1). This document does not restate that script's copy or reasoning — it references it by beat/shot number throughout. Read that document first, specifically the Campaign B section and its Beat 1 remediation block in full before reading this one.

**This is a full redesign (v2), not a patch of the prior draft.** The prior version of this file (git history) storyboarded the pre-redesign script (single-card Hook, feature-labeled Beat 2 captions) using a static-screenshot/Pillow/ffmpeg-concat production method, and was assembled into a first draft the Product Owner judged creatively weak: "a narrated slideshow, not an advertisement that stops someone from scrolling." Three named weaknesses are mine to fix in this pass — **(3) very little motion or visual energy, (4) no overlays/callouts/emphasis guiding the eye, (5) feels like a demo instead of a compelling story** — weaknesses 1/2/6 were `marketing`'s and are already resolved in the revised script this document builds against. This redesign changes both the **creative treatment** (motion, overlays, narrative structure) and the **production method** (real screen-recorded video via Playwright, assembled in Remotion, replacing static screenshots assembled via Pillow/ffmpeg) — the two are linked: several of the weakness-3/5 fixes below are only possible because the production method changed, not available under the old method.

**Owner of this pass:** `ui-designer`, per Main's dispatch — storyboard/shot-list/motion-plan only. **Scope note, stated plainly:** this document does not render, capture, or produce anything. No video, no audio, no frame exists yet from this pass. It goes back through the same review loop (`ux-critic` → `brand-guardian` verification of the open items below → `reviewer`) before any new draft is produced, per the task's own instruction.

## 0. What changed, in one paragraph

The Hook (Beat 1) is restructured per the script's own two-card structure — "En el bazar…" (screen-free) → "…¿se te va el cliente mientras intentas anotar la venta?" (product reveal) — with the primary-text placement moved to the upper third of frame (evidence-based placement convention; v1 had it in a band below the screenshot, which this redesign corrects). Beat 2's three redesigned value captions get word-highlighted ("karaoke") caption treatment, numbered sequence badges on the setup montage only, and — the single biggest unlock from the production-method change — **real recorded tap/scroll motion instead of static held stills**, including a real two-tap sequence in shot 4 that v1 explicitly could not show. Beat 3 and Beat 4b, both single static holds well over the ~5-second retention-risk threshold in v1, are each split into a two-part sequential reveal of the *same* locked copy so no single frame sits static that long. Numbered/sequential overlays, emphasis rings, and word-highlight captions are the guided-eye devices addressing weakness 4. The whole thing is reframed structurally as one continuous narrative beat (problem → watch-it-happen → honest pause → invitation) rather than a screen-by-screen feature tour, addressing weakness 5. Production method: Playwright `recordVideo` (real captured video of real interactions against the live prototype) as raw material, Remotion (React-based composition) as the assembly/caption/overlay/motion layer — replacing static Playwright screenshots + Pillow captions + ffmpeg concat.

## 1. What this draft is, and isn't — read this before the shot list

This redesign changes what's true here relative to v1, in both directions — some limitations v1 had to disclose are now genuinely resolved; a new one is introduced.

**Now genuinely real, that v1 could not show:**
- **Real tap motion.** Playwright's automated `.click()`/`.tap()` calls, captured live via `recordVideo`, produce a real state transition in real time — a tile actually highlighting, a running total actually updating, a screen actually navigating — not a hard cut between two stills.
- **Real scrolling**, where the live prototype scrolls, at its actual scroll behavior/easing.
- **Real transition motion**, whatever the live prototype itself renders on state change (React re-render, any existing CSS transition) — captured as it actually happens, not invented for the video. If the prototype renders no transition on a given state change (a plain instant swap), that's what gets captured; no transition is added in Remotion that the app itself doesn't have — that would misrepresent the product, the same Product Truth discipline v1 already held itself to for content.
- **Match-cut continuity** between consecutive shots, because real cursor/tap coordinates are now known and reproducible — a technique unavailable when every shot was an independently-composited still.

**Still not real, disclosed here rather than left implicit:**
- **No real human finger.** Playwright automation has no hand on a phone. This draft uses a synthetic tap-indicator overlay (see Judgment call SB2-8) composited in Remotion — a small circular pulse rendered at the real, actual tap coordinate Playwright fires the event at. This is a production technique, not a claim about who is using the app; it does not misrepresent anything the script or Beat 4's disclosure already covers.
- **Voiceover is still synthesized**, not a live actor, per the brief's "one voice throughout." **Voice source is resolved (2026-08-23, superseding the 2026-08-20 Antonio decision below): Azure Neural TTS, voice = Marina (es-MX, Mexican-accent female).** See §7 for the full investigation history (Paulina/Mónica/Reed → Antonio/Erika → Marina) and §8.6 for status — unlike Antonio, this voice carries no known pending commercial-use gate, since it was generated on a paid (S0) Azure Speech resource from the start.
- **The prototype itself is still a prototype.** Nothing about a better recording method changes Beat 4's Product Truth disclosure obligations — that disclosure still covers the product, not the video's own production method.

**A real, load-bearing consequence of the method change, now resolved by `brand-guardian`:** v1's Beat 4 review round (its own §6, item (c)) added an on-screen disclosure to the Hook frame, cleared by `brand-guardian`: **"Capturas reales del prototipo — no es una grabación."** That line is now factually **false** under this production method — it genuinely *is* a recording. Carrying that exact caption forward into this redesign would have introduced a new, self-inflicted Product Truth problem this pass would have been responsible for. This was flagged as an open item for `brand-guardian`, which has since resolved it: **remove the caption entirely, do not replace it with new wording.** Its reasoning: the caption existed to disclose that v1's static-screenshot compositing wasn't real continuous footage — under the new Playwright `recordVideo` method, it genuinely is real footage, so there's nothing left to disclose at Beat 1. Beat 4's existing "Este es un prototipo interactivo" disclosure already carries the substantive Product Truth obligation (what the product is, not how the video was made); duplicating a weaker version at Beat 1 adds nothing and costs real reading time inside the already-tight ≤5s hook window. **Beat 1 carries no on-screen disclosure caption, by design, not by omission** — `brand-guardian`'s own wording, recorded here verbatim. No disclosure caption appears anywhere in Beat 1 of this storyboard, per that resolution.

## 2. Judgment calls this redesign depends on, stated up front

**Judgment call SB2-1 — production method, stated once, applies to every shot below.** Raw material: Playwright `recordVideo` (browser-context option) or `page.video()`, capturing real interaction sequences against the live prototype (`npm run dev`, plain build — not `dev:demo-campaign`, same reasoning as v1: Campaign B's shots never touch the Demo Mode welcome screen). `AppState` reached by real interaction exactly as a merchant would, same disclosed convention as v1 (`product/02c-high-fidelity-prototype/README.md`), with the same one seeding exception carried forward (`currentUser.phoneVerifiedAt` pre-seeded to skip Authentication's phone/OTP screens, which this script never shows). Assembly: Remotion (React-based video composition) — each shot is a `<Sequence>`; recorded clips are `<Video>` components trimmed to the needed window; montage speed-ramps use Remotion's `playbackRate`; captions/overlays are plain React components absolutely positioned over the video layer, using the project's actual brand tokens (Fredoka for display/statement text, Inter for running captions, the same type-scale and color tokens as `company/brand/brand-guide.md`) rather than Pillow-rendered PNGs composited frame-by-frame. Final export: H.264, 1080×1920 (9:16), 30fps, matching v1's target format.

**Judgment call SB2-2 — recording resolution, not screenshot-scale tricks.** v1 captured screenshots at viewport 430×932 @3x device-scale-factor to get sharp stills at output resolution. `recordVideo` doesn't honor `deviceScaleFactor` the same way static screenshot capture does — video output pixel dimensions follow the viewport directly. **Recommendation for the next production pass, not resolved here since nothing is captured in this pass:** set the Playwright viewport directly to the target output width (e.g. 1080px-equivalent, or as close as the prototype's responsive breakpoints allow) rather than relying on a scale factor, to avoid soft/upscaled video. Flagged as a technical decision for whoever runs the actual capture, not decided by this storyboard.

**Judgment call SB2-3 — the montage still has to pass through the same two real screens v1's SB-1/SB-2 identified, and that finding is unchanged.** The underlying Approved specs (`onboarding.md`, `inventory.md`) haven't changed since v1: reaching a sellable Home (Idle) genuinely requires the business-identity capture step (`onboarding.md §3.9`, "Tu negocio") and a real stock-registration pass (`inventory.md §3.6–§3.10`, "Registro de mercancía") — neither is optional, neither appears in the script's own literal shot table. v1's SB-1/SB-2 reasoning for including both, folded into the disclosed "visibly sped-up, jump-cut" montage treatment, carries forward unchanged; not re-derived here. What changes in this redesign is *how* that montage is shot (real sped-up recorded footage, not held stills) and *how it's dressed* (numbered badges — see SB2-6) — not which screens it passes through or why.

**Judgment call SB2-4 — Beat 1's primary text moves from below the screenshot (v1) to the upper third of frame, above it.** The knowledge-mentor evidence's placement convention is explicit: primary/emphasis text in a consistent upper-third-or-center zone, *running* captions lower-third above UI chrome — two different zones for two different jobs. v1 put Beat 1's hook caption in a band below the screenshot crop, which is the *running-caption* zone, not the *primary-claim* zone — a placement mismatch v1 didn't have reason to catch since the evidence base wasn't yet in hand at that time. This redesign corrects it: card 1b's text sits in the upper third, the product-screen crop occupies the frame beneath it. This also resolves cleanly for card 1a (screen-free, so the text is simply centered/upper-third with nothing competing for space) and keeps Beat 2's shot 3/4/5 captions correctly in the lower-third running-caption zone, which was already right in v1 and stays unchanged.

**Judgment call SB2-5 — card 1b's line appears as a single unit, never staggered, per the script's own explicit constraint.** The script's Beat 1 remediation addendum is unambiguous and binding: "the full grounded line in card 1b must appear as a single unit, or, if revealed progressively at all, must reveal in an order that never leaves the loss/outcome clause on screen alone, even transiently... it is specifically not a legitimate way to split card 1b's own line." This storyboard specifies **single-unit reveal for card 1b, full stop** — one Remotion `<Sequence>`, one pop-in of the entire line at once (150–250ms fade/scale-in, no stagger, no per-word timing). To still give this line some guided-eye emphasis (weakness 4) without violating the constraint, this redesign applies a **static, non-temporal color treatment within the single reveal**: one or two words within the line (e.g. "cliente" / "venta") render in Coral AA+ against the rest in Obsidian/white, and the *entire* line — accent color included — appears together, at the same instant, as one block. This is emphasis by contrast, not emphasis by timing, so it does not create the isolation risk the constraint exists to prevent. Word-by-word karaoke captions (legitimate everywhere else in this script, see SB2-9) are explicitly **not used** on card 1b for this reason.

**Judgment call SB2-6 — numbered sequence badges apply to the setup montage only, not to the payload shots.** The evidence base's numbered/sequential-overlay finding is scoped to "feature-listicle content, where applicable." Beat 2's setup montage (shots 1, 1a, 2, 2a — Empezar gratis → Tu negocio → Define lo que vendes → Registro de mercancía) genuinely is a short listicle of setup steps, and a small corner badge (1/4 → 4/4, pill-shaped per the Design System's existing Badges convention, Coral AA+ fill) increases watch-through exactly the way the evidence describes. The payload shots (3, 4, 5 — the actual selling demonstration) are not a listicle; they're the emotional/story core this video actually sells. Badging them the same way would flatten the story into a checklist, working directly against weakness-5's fix rather than for it — so they carry no numbered badge, only the value captions and their own overlay treatment (SB2-7 below).

**Judgment call SB2-7 — shot 4 shows a real two-tap sequence, not a static after-state; this reverses v1's own explicit framing note, and that reversal is deliberate and disclosed.** v1's shot-4 framing note stated: "with no live tap motion available, I show only the resulting state... rather than a sequence of intermediate single-item states." That constraint no longer holds — `recordVideo` genuinely captures the real two-tap sequence in real time. This redesign reverses that call: shot 4 shows the real, unbroken action of tapping two product tiles in sequence (Playeras, then Blusas), each tap producing its own real running-total update and its own brief overlay cue (a ~150–250ms scale/flash on the updated total, plus a small "+$220"/"+$320" pop-in chip near each tapped tile as it registers — restrained per-element cues, not kinetic typography, timed to the real tap events rather than invented). This is simultaneously the single largest fix for weakness 3 (it's genuine motion, not a held frame) and for weakness 5 (a viewer watches an action happen rather than being shown its result) available anywhere in this shot list, and it exists only because the production method changed — flagged explicitly as the clearest example of the "these two changes are linked" point made in §0.

**Judgment call SB2-8 — synthetic tap-indicator overlay, disclosed as a production technique.** Since Playwright automation fires events with no visible human hand, this draft renders a small circular pulse (Coral AA+, ~150–250ms fade in/out, matching the evidence's restrained-motion guidance) at each real tap's actual screen coordinate, composited in Remotion. It marks *where* an action happens, not *who* performs it, and introduces no claim beyond what Beat 4's existing Product Truth disclosure already covers. Named explicitly rather than left as an invisible production detail, the same disclosure discipline v1 applied to its own synthesized voice and non-recorded stills.

**Judgment call SB2-9 — Beat 2's three redesigned value captions (shots 3, 4, 5) use word-highlight ("karaoke") timing; shots 1/1a/2/2a (silent or short connective captions) don't.** The evidence base's sound-off-by-default finding calls specifically for claims that matter to be visually legible without audio — that's exactly what shots 3–5's redesigned captions are (already `brand-guardian`-cleared copy: "Lista para vender, sin perder tiempo," "Sin perder al cliente," "Y la venta, ya quedó guardada"). Each renders as running lower-third text with the actively-spoken word in Coral AA+ against Obsidian/white for the rest, timed to the voiceover's own word boundaries. Shots 1/1a/2/2a's captions are short connective tissue ("Ya armaste tu negocio…," none) already covered by v1's own reasoning that they're outcome-framed, not feature-framed — they get simple fade/pop-in (no karaoke), keeping the montage's visual rhythm fast and uncluttered rather than adding a caption technique to shots that don't need it.

**Judgment call SB2-10 — Beat 3 and Beat 4b are each split into a two-part sequential reveal of the same locked copy, to break a single-static-frame hold that otherwise exceeds the ~5-second retention-risk threshold.** Beat 3's hypothesis line (6.5s total hold) and Beat 4b's CTA-plus-incentive card (6.0s total hold) are both, in v1, a single unbroken static image held well past the evidence base's named ~5-second retention-risk line — and neither line's wording is available to shorten (Beat 3 is Product-Owner-authored verbatim copy; Beat 4b's CTA and incentive lines are locked, per the script's own repeated "not part of this refinement's scope" framing). The fix doesn't touch the words — it splits *when* pre-existing clauses become visible, so the frame's visible content changes partway through an otherwise-static hold:
- **Beat 3:** "Estamos probando si registrar una venta puede ser tan rápido..." appears first (pop-in), held to roughly the natural spoken clause break (~3.1s), then "...que no te haga perder al siguiente cliente." pop-in completes the sentence, both clauses remaining visible together for the remainder of the 6.5s total hold. Same background (Balanced #F4F4F4), same total words, same total hold time — no new claim, no new visual scene, just a mid-hold content change.
- **Beat 4b:** the CTA line ("Pruébalo y cuéntanos si esto te serviría — las dos cosas nos ayudan por igual") pop-in first, held alone for roughly the length of its own voiceover (4.77s); the incentive subtext pop-in beneath it after that, remaining together for the balance of the 6.0s total hold. This also reinforces weakness-4's guided-eye goal directly — a viewer's attention lands on the ask first, then the supporting incentive, in the order that actually matters, rather than both competing for attention from frame one.

Neither split is a progressive/staggered *word-level* reveal (the technique the script explicitly reserves out of card 1b only) — both are a two-clause sequential reveal at the sentence level, which the script's own addendum confirms remains "a legitimate motion technique everywhere else in this script." Confirmed applicable here since neither Beat 3 nor Beat 4b is card 1b.

**Judgment call SB2-11 — framing/crop is now a live video crop, not a stacked-image composite, which structurally eliminates v1's seam-defect class.** v1's SB-3 composited two separate screenshot crops (top content + bottom action-button region) to avoid showing empty scroll space — and that composite seam is exactly what caused the five defects `ux-critic`'s independent review found and fixed in v1 §6(b) (stray line fragments, clipped glyphs, a cut-through button). Under the new method, the same "skip the empty scroll middle, keep the button visible" framing goal is achieved with a **live crop/mask of one continuous recorded frame** — there are two regions of interest within a single unbroken video layer, not two separately-sourced images stacked together, so there is no seam to defect. Worth naming explicitly as a real, structural benefit of the method change, not just a stylistic one.

## 3. Narrative structure — the story this shot list is built to tell (weakness 5)

Stated once, up front, since it governs every shot choice below rather than being visible in any single row of the shot table: this redesign treats Beat 1→2 as one continuous beat — *a merchant's own moment, watched, not narrated* — rather than a hook followed by an independent feature tour. Card 1a states her situation in four words with nothing else on screen. Card 1b names the exact friction and, in the same instant, shows her actual screen — the reveal is motivated by the words, not a cut to "now here's the app." The setup montage (shots 1/1a/2/2a) reads as "watch her get ready, fast" (numbered, sped-up, real recorded motion) rather than a checklist of screens — the same footage v1 could only render as a slideshow now genuinely moves. Shot 4's real two-tap sequence is the story's payoff moment — the viewer watches the exact thing the hook asked about (losing a customer to slow registration) *not happen*, in real time, rather than being told it didn't happen via a caption over a static "after" frame. Beat 3's pause and Beat 4's honest disclosure-then-invitation close the arc without breaking it — same neutral card language as the rest of the script, no tonal jump into "ad voice."
