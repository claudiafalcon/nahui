### Q11 — What is the specific per-transition timing rule for self-service Business Capability changes (immediate vs. deferred), and what does "deferred" concretely mean?

- **Raised by:** Q5's resolution (below) — the general mechanism (self-service, bidirectional, business-rule-governed timing) is now decided; the specific rule per transition is not.
- **Question:** Of the four directions across the two self-service capabilities (Free↔Paid, Loyalty Enabled↔Disabled), which take effect immediately and which are deferred, and for any deferred transition, what determines the effective date? The Product Owner's own resolution gave one illustrative example — "a Paid → Free downgrade may become effective at the end of the current billing period" — but explicitly as an example ("may become"), not a settled rule for every transition. **Narrowed by `decision-log.md` D27**: NFC capability is no longer an independently self-service-toggleable direction — it's a read-time derivation from `subscriptionTier`, so its "timing" is just whatever `subscriptionTier`'s own transition timing resolves to, not a separate question. `defaultSellingMode` (also newly self-service per D27) isn't in scope for this question either — it's immediate-only by definition, not a commercial/billing capability.
- **Why this can't be answered yet:** the illustrative example presupposes a billing-cycle concept that doesn't exist anywhere in the current Foundation. `company/CLAUDE.md`'s business model direction states pricing is "not final" ("Flat/seasonal pricing instead of monthly if usage isn't monthly-constant"), and payments/checkout stays an explicit non-goal. This question is blocked on that pricing/billing model being designed first — not a domain-modeling gap, a genuinely unresolved business-model question.
- **What's already resolved, not reopened by this item:** the domain *supports* both immediate and deferred effect uniformly (`decision-log.md` D25) — this question is only about which specific transitions use which, and the deferred timing's exact rule.
- **Referenced from:** `decision-log.md` D25; will be referenced from `product/02-ux/settings.md`'s pending-change UI once designed.
- **Status:** Open, explicitly deferred — **capability-change timing cannot be resolved until a pricing model and billing-cycle policy exist in the Foundation**, and neither does today. This is not a gap in analysis; it's waiting on a business-model decision that hasn't been made yet, independent of anything UX or Medium-Fidelity work does.
- **Does not block current work.** `settings.md`'s deferred-change UI is already built to support either immediate-with-confirmation or deferred-with-indicator per capability (§2.2/§3.4/§3.5), with today's assignment (e.g., "Volver al plan gratis" shown as deferred, illustrative date "14 de agosto") explicitly marked illustrative, not final. Do not let this question block any UX, Medium-Fidelity, or build-readiness work going forward — **the one exception is if a specific screen ever needs to state a concrete billing promise as fact** (an exact date, a specific proration rule, a specific "you'll be charged X on Y" commitment) rather than an illustrative placeholder; that specific screen would need this resolved first, everything else does not.

### Q12 — Primary-button color fails WCAG AA contrast; brand color choice needs a decision

- **Raised by:** `events.md`'s Medium-Fidelity Figma build (`product/02b-medium-fidelity/events.md`) and its `ux-critic`/`reviewer` review cycle, 2026-08-02.
- **Question:** `company/brand/brand-guide.md`'s own defined Primary-button treatment — solid Coral (`#E86248`) fill, white text — computes to ~3.35:1 contrast, below WCAG AA's 4.5:1 minimum for normal-size text (and only marginally clears the 3:1 large-text exception, itself unconfirmed for this button's actual type size). This is every Primary CTA across the product, not one screen — first became concretely visible once real color started rendering in Medium-Fidelity work, but it's a brand-wide definition, not a per-document bug. **Scope confirmed wider during `home.md`'s Medium-Fidelity review**: `brand-guide.md`'s Tertiary/link treatment ("text-only, Coral") uses the identical Coral value as plain text on white/light backgrounds — WCAG contrast is symmetric regardless of which color is foreground vs. background, so this computes to the same ~3.35:1 failure. This is one underlying color decision surfacing in two places (Primary-button fill and Tertiary/link text), not two separate issues. **A third, related instance surfaced during `settings.md`'s Medium-Fidelity `ux-critic` review, 2026-08-02**: the Secondary/outlined-button treatment (border + text, no fill) renders in a deep maroon that's the same hex as `color/tezontle-dark` — visually near-identical to the Error/Destructive accent — and `settings.md` uses it as its dominant, repeated CTA language for what its own spec frames as low-stakes, reversible actions ("Activating something should cost her almost nothing"). Not a WCAG contrast failure like the other two — a tone/semantic-clarity concern instead (a reversible, low-stakes action visually reading as a cautionary one). Confirmed as pre-existing, inherited Design System behavior (`home.md`'s "Ver detalle" button already uses the identical treatment), not something `settings.md` introduced — but `settings.md` is the first document to lean on it this heavily. Flagged alongside Q12 rather than as its own item since it's the same underlying color-system question (is Coral/Tezontle-dark doing too many jobs at once) rather than a separate one.
- **Why this needs a Business Decision, not just a design fix:** changing Coral itself, or the paired button text color, is a brand-identity change with real tradeoffs (visual identity consistency vs. accessibility compliance) — not something a single UX/UI remediation pass should resolve unilaterally by overriding the canonical brand definition. Options include darkening Coral for button-fill contexts specifically (the same approach already applied to fix related contrast issues via `color/tezontle-dark`), relying on a confirmed large-text exception if the button type size genuinely qualifies, or accepting the tradeoff deliberately with a documented rationale.
- **What's already resolved, not reopened by this item:** the fix pattern for *adjacent* contrast issues (text/border treatments, a pill's text-on-fill combination) — rebinding to `color/tezontle-dark` — is already applied and verified where it didn't touch the brand's core Primary-button definition itself.
- **Referenced from:** `product/02b-medium-fidelity/events.md`'s "Known gaps" section.
- **Resolution (Product Owner, 2026-08-02):** Introduce a new token, **Coral AA+ (`#C13F26`, 5.24:1 vs. white — passes WCAG AA with real margin)**, used specifically where text/fill contrast is load-bearing: Primary-button fill (white text on top) and Tertiary/link text on white/light backgrounds. The original Coral (`#E86248`) is unchanged as the brand's reference hue for non-text-critical uses. Chosen over reusing the existing `color/tezontle-dark` token (accessible at 6.91:1, but visually reads as maroon and would collide with the Destructive/Error semantic already assigned to that token) and over the tighter minimal-shift alternative Coral AA (`#CE4935`, 4.54:1 — clears AA by too thin a margin to be safe across real device rendering variance).
- **Not resolved by this item, tracked as its own concern:** the Secondary/outlined-button maroon-tone tone/semantic-clarity question raised alongside Q12 (third instance, above) — that's a separate design-language question about whether Tezontle Dark should keep double-duty as both Secondary-button-outline and Destructive/Error, not a contrast failure Coral AA+ fixes.
- **Applied and verified.** `company/brand/brand-guide.md`'s Colors section (new Coral AA+ entry). Figma rollout complete via a single component-level fix — new variable `color/coral-aa` (`#C13F26`) in the `Nahui/Colors` collection, rebound on the shared `Button` component's Primary fill and Tertiary text (not a per-document change). `ux-critic` independently verified via a direct instance-level sweep across all six Medium-Fidelity documents: zero wrong-colored instances found anywhere; Secondary/Destructive/NavBar/decorative-Coral (chevrons, teaser links) all confirmed genuinely untouched. One Minor process note (not a design defect): the build report's own self-counted Tertiary-instance tallies were inaccurate on 2 of 6 pages, though every instance found was still correctly colored — see `company/infrastructure-decisions.md` ID001 addendum.
- **Status:** Resolved.

- **Original question:** The provisional cloud architecture (`evidence/2026-08-12-innovation-accounting/innovation-accounting-draft.md` §2a) named Web NFC (`NDEFReader`) as the read mechanism, with an open caveat: it works on Chrome/Android but not iOS Safari. The Product Owner's explicit business requirement, given directly rather than a device-target pick: **Ana must never need to buy a separate reader — her own phone is the only hardware in the loop** — and asked for a joint Architect + Knowledge Mentor investigation into the actual best solution meeting that constraint, not a forced choice between the options already named.
- **Architecture matter — resolved, not RFC-worthy.** `architect` recommends, and confirmed via a `knowledge-mentor` consultation against current external sources (caniuse.com, MDN, WebKit's own standards-positions tracker, Apple's DMA compliance page — no reversal on iOS Web NFC support found, no relevant DMA carve-out): keep Android on Web NFC inside the existing React/TypeScript PWA (zero new distribution surface), and wrap the same codebase in a thin native shell for iOS with a Core NFC bridge for tag reads, rather than building a second, fully separate native app. `registrationMode`/NFC-entitlement modeling (`decision-log.md` D23, D27) is already client-platform-agnostic and needs no change either way.
- **Resolution (Product Owner, 2026-08-12):**

  - **iOS:** shared React/TypeScript codebase with the thin native/Core NFC bridge, as recommended — no change.
  - **iOS pilot distribution:** **TestFlight**, with selected merchants. Product Owner has direct prior experience with TestFlight and is comfortable with this approach. This resolves what "Commitment B" below had left open — pilot-scale distribution stays private/invitation-only, no public App Store listing needed for the pilot.
  - **Apple Developer membership:** the Product Owner **already has an account**; it's currently expired, so no new enrollment is needed. Renew when the iOS pilot actually starts, not before.
  - **No public App Store launch decision required for the pilot** — deferred until it's actually relevant (post-pilot scale-up), not decided here.

### Q13 — Should the Merchant Experience Kit maintain a full parallel Spanish page, and if so, what keeps it in sync?

- **Raised by:** `reviewer`'s Foundation-consistency review of the Merchant Experience Kit FigJam board (2026-08-05), which declined to rule on this itself and asked it be routed to `architect`; `architect` then classified it here rather than resolving it unilaterally.
- **Question:** The Merchant Experience Kit (a FigJam board — Persona Canvas, Empathy Map, Merchant Journey, Design Principles, Experience Checklist — meant to be the primary internal reference for future design work) has two full, independently-built pages: an English page and a complete Spanish translation, built at the Product Owner's request. Nothing records *why* an internal, agent/team-facing reference artifact needs a full bilingual copy, who actually reads the Spanish page directly, or what mechanism (if any) keeps the two pages from drifting apart once both are hand-editable.
- **Why this needs a Business Decision, not just a design fix:** `architect` ruled that, as written, `global-principles.md`'s Product Language section scopes English to "documentation... and all developer/agent-facing artifacts" and Spanish to "anything Ana sees or reads" — this internal artifact defaults to English under that rule, and the existing "authentic merchant vocabulary" clause covers embedding Ana's own quoted words in Spanish, not translating the whole document. A second, independently-edited page is therefore an exception to a settled principle, not something the principle already accommodates — and `architect` correctly declined to invent the business reason for that exception itself (same Decision Ownership constraint that applies to Main). Whether the exception is warranted is a call about who this internal artifact actually serves and whether that's worth an ongoing maintenance cost — an operations question, not a product-behavior or Foundation-interpretation one.
- **Evidence the risk is real, not theoretical:** a real content bug (SIENTE-quadrant phrasing violating Product Language rules) existed identically on both pages and was only caught by accident, during an unrelated fix, via a manual cross-check — nothing currently guarantees a fix to one page reaches the other.
- **Options, per `architect`'s ruling:** (1) retire the Spanish page entirely, keeping Ana's authentic quotes embedded inline in the single English artifact — satisfies the language principle and eliminates drift risk with zero sync mechanism needed; (2) keep both, but make the Spanish page mechanically generated/derived from the English source and never hand-edited directly; (3) state an explicit business reason for maintaining two independently-editable pages and accept manual-parity discipline as a standing cost — `architect` notes this option already failed once and isn't recommended without a stronger process than "check both when either is touched."
- **What's already resolved, not reopened by this item:** the specific SIENTE-quadrant content bug itself — fixed and verified identically on both pages, independent of what happens to the pages' structure going forward.
- **Referenced from:** `company/bitacora.md` (Merchant Experience Kit entries); the Kit's FigJam board itself (fileKey `yjb7sUjdueUfzGKHmJHKhy`).
- **Status:** Open. Blocks the Kit's "Approved / primary-reference" sign-off until answered — `reviewer` and `ux-designer` are to treat the Spanish page as not independently authoritative in the meantime, with any future content fix applied and verified on both pages explicitly, not left to accidental catch-up.

### Q5 — Are Business Capabilities ever merchant-self-service-editable after onboarding?

- **Original question:** Is `registrationMode`/`subscriptionTier`/`loyaltyEnabled` fixed for a Business's lifetime once chosen, or can Ana ever change one herself in-app? Narrowed by Q4's resolution to specifically *whether a capability can ever change after onboarding completes, and by what mechanism* — self-service in-app, or only via backend/support.
- **First evaluated, not resolved, alongside RFC 0003:** `product/99-rfc/0003-session-selling-mode.md` (Accepted; `decision-log.md` D23) was considered as a possible alternative resolution path, then accepted on its own domain-modeling merits without deciding this question — it assumed a Business's capability set *could* change post-onboarding (e.g., `nfc` revoked by a subscription lapse) without saying how or by whom.
- **Resolution (Product Owner):** Business Capabilities are **fully self-service, in both directions, at any time** — `subscriptionTier` (Free↔Paid), `loyaltyEnabled` (Enabled↔Disabled), and `registrationMode`'s `nfc` entitlement (Enabled↔Disabled) can all be changed by the merchant herself, subject to two invariants: changing a capability never deletes historical data, and business rules — not always "immediate" — determine when a given change actually takes effect (e.g., a downgrade might land at a future point rather than instantly).
- **How the analysis got here:** Architect first confirmed (in a round that also considered activation-only as a narrower option) that D23's NFC Readiness mechanism fully absorbs the original concern that made `registrationMode` uniquely risky to self-service (orphaned untagged inventory after a Buttons→NFC switch) — that risk no longer exists once NFC Readiness's graceful Ready/Limited Ready/Not Ready degradation exists, regardless of when or how the capability was granted. When the Product Owner then extended the resolution to cover deactivation too, plus business-rule-governed (not always immediate) timing, a second Architect round confirmed: the deferred-timing case needs one small addition (an optional pending-value + effective-date pair per capability, so a deferred change is honestly displayable — not a new aggregate, bounded context, or ubiquitous-language redefinition), the "never delete historical data" guarantee was already structurally true for all three capabilities in both directions (never newly built, just newly stated explicitly), and `Session.operatingMode`'s existing D23 immutability guarantee needed no change to accommodate deferred timing.
- **Applied:** `decision-log.md` D25 (plain entry, not RFC — same category as D19). `domain-model.md`'s Business Capabilities table gains the pending-value/effective-date structural note for all three capabilities and states the never-delete invariant explicitly.
- **Explicitly not resolved here, tracked separately:** the *specific* per-transition timing rule (which of the six directions is immediate vs. deferred, and the exact deferred rule) depends on a pricing/billing-cycle model that doesn't exist in the Foundation yet — tracked as Q11, above, not silently assumed from the Product Owner's own illustrative example.
- **Unblocks:** `product/02-ux/settings.md` — six merchant-facing actions (three capabilities × two directions), a reusable "pending change, takes effect on [date]" indicator, and a way to cancel a pending change before it lands.
- **Status:** Superseded by `decision-log.md` D40 (2026-08-09) — `loyaltyEnabled` is retired outright; this resolution's inclusion of `loyaltyEnabled` among the self-service-toggleable capabilities no longer applies, and `settings.md`'s action count narrows from six to four. `subscriptionTier`'s self-service treatment, described above, is unaffected and remains Resolved in substance.

### Q8 — Does paid-tier customer segmentation require merchant-visible customer identity, and how does that reconcile with Loyalty-claim's merchant-app boundary?

- **Original question:** `company/CLAUDE.md`'s core thesis and `backlog.md` #2 frame customer segmentation as a paid-tier, merchant-facing Resultados feature. But `domain-model.md`'s bounded-context table put Customer identity entirely inside Loyalty-claim, with no path for Ana's app to ever know two Sales came from the same repeat customer. The "Tus clientes" screen (`reports.md` §3.12) was built as an explicitly-flagged illustrative placeholder pending this decision.
- **Resolution (Product Owner):** **Path (a) — the complete capability, chosen over narrowing scope.** Customer Segmentation is a **core product capability**, not an NFC-specific one. Customer Identity belongs exclusively to the Loyalty platform; the Merchant Application consumes only **Derived Customer Intelligence** (an anonymized, aggregate signal — never raw Customer/Claim data). **Claim is a single business capability** (`loyaltyEnabled`) resolvable through multiple mechanisms — an NFC tag scan (existing), a Sale-level **Claim Token** displayed as a QR at Sale finalization (new — no manual code entry, the customer just scans it and lands in the Loyalty-claim flow), and future mechanisms. `registrationMode` determines which mechanism a given Sale uses, never whether the capability exists.
- **How the analysis got here:** three rounds of joint Planner (business-impact) + Architect (domain-implications) analysis. Round 1 (original NFC-only framing) found Path (a) matched the validated friction but had a real coverage gap (permanently excluded paid Buttons-mode merchants). Round 2 (correcting the NFC-gating assumption) confirmed the gap could close in principle but added real new scope (a second claim mechanism needing its own design/validation). Round 3 (the concrete Sale-QR mechanism) confirmed the adoption-risk concern was resolved by the QR-scan pattern, and Architect gave a definitive ruling: a Sale-level Claim Token scan resolves into N independent SaleItem-level Claims for a multi-item Sale — architecturally identical in shape to how a multi-item NFC Sale already produces N independent claims. All three of the Product Owner's framing questions (one capability; multiple mechanisms; one customer experience) were confirmed clean and consistent with the Foundation across this process.
- **Wording correction along the way:** the original "zero merchant IA presence" / "no entry point anywhere in the merchant app" phrasing was found to be genuinely ambiguous (does the merchant app *displaying* a claim artifact count as an "entry point"?) and was corrected — see `decision-log.md` D21. The Merchant Application may display artifacts (tag, QR, receipt, future mechanism); it just never performs identification or runs any part of the Loyalty experience.
- **Applied:** `product/99-rfc/0002-loyalty-claim-complete-capability.md` (Accepted); `domain-model.md` (bounded-context table — Loyalty-claim gains a read-only Selling dependency, Intelligence gains a read-only Loyalty-claim dependency; new "Multi-mechanism Claim resolution" key mechanism); `ubiquitous-language.md` (Claim and Customer definitions generalized; two new terms, Claim Token and Derived Customer Intelligence); `decision-log.md` D22.
- **Implementation sequencing — explicitly separate from this decision, per the Product Owner's instruction:** the Foundation now describes the complete target capability; staged build order (which mechanism ships first) is tracked in `company/backlog.md` #2, not here.
- **Applied:** `reports.md` §3.6/§3.12/§3.13 ("Tus clientes") was redesigned as a real spec against this resolved architecture — gated by `subscriptionTier=paid` and `loyaltyEnabled=true` together, consuming only Derived Customer Intelligence — and completed its own `ux-designer` → `ux-critic` → `reviewer` pass (zero Blockers/unresolved Majors). See `product/02-ux/ux-critic-findings.md`'s "Customer Segmentation (Q8) resolution cycle" entry.
- **Status:** Resolved.

### Q4 — Who/what sets `registrationMode` (and other capabilities) on first run?

- **Question:** A Business must have its initial capabilities (`registrationMode` at minimum) set before Home's resolution logic has anything to resolve. Is this Ana self-selecting during onboarding, implied by a purchase decision, or something else?

- **Architect finding:** Adopted — this resolves Q4 more cleanly than the original framing, not merely relocating it: it's directly required by `global-principles.md`'s "business language before technical language" and "never ask twice" (an isolated `registrationMode: buttons|nfc` picker would have violated both). Structurally correct per `architecture-principles.md` #1 (decide once, at the highest level) and consistent with D13's existing Onboarding sequencing. `domain-model.md`'s Business Capabilities table needs no change — it already, correctly, only describes read-time behavior and was always silent on the write/set mechanism.
- **Payments/checkout non-goal clarification (important, recorded explicitly so it isn't inferred later from onboarding copy):** the "Activate an NFC Starter Kit" path must be modeled as confirming possession of an already-obtained physical kit (e.g., entering an activation code) — **zero in-app payment processing**. Any future in-app purchase flow for the kit would reverse the explicit `company/CLAUDE.md` non-goal ("Payments/checkout — out of scope") and requires its own separate Business Decision, not something that arrives implicitly via an onboarding flow name. - **Applied:** `decision-log.md` D19 records this decision. No `domain-model.md` change needed. Exact onboarding paths, copy, and count are deferred to Onboarding UX design (`ux-designer`'s job, not decided here).
- **Status:** Resolved.
