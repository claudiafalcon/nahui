# Product Decisions

Standing log of open questions classified as **Product Decisions** under the Decision Ownership policy in `company/CLAUDE.md`: questions that change product behavior, user experience, capabilities, or feature scope, and require a Product Owner call — not something Architect can resolve by interpreting the existing `product/00-foundation/`, and not a pricing/commercialization/legal/operations matter.

This file was created by reclassifying entries that previously lived in `product/02-ux/architect-questions.md`, per the new Decision Ownership policy. **Content and status are unchanged from the original entries** — this is a governance move, not a reassessment. Each entry's original Architect finding is preserved verbatim below; it's still the reasoning behind why each item is open, even though the item itself is no longer an Architect Decision.

Entries are never deleted once resolved; mark them Resolved with the outcome instead, so the history of what was ambiguous and why stays intact (same non-deletion rule as `product/99-rfc/`). A resolved Product Decision that changes the domain model, ubiquitous language, or IA should still go through an RFC (`product/99-rfc/`) before `product/00-foundation/` is touched, per existing policy — this log is the queue feeding that process, not a replacement for it.

### Q22 — Should the replayed trial Sale (Q19's conversion moment) be classified separately from genuine merchant activity, and how should it appear in Results?

- **Question, raised by the Product Owner (2026-09-03):** Q19's conversion moment replays the pre-auth trial as a genuine, persisted Sale so the merchant doesn't lose the "sale → Results → history" value loop she just experienced. But that Sale happened against sample products, not her real catalog — silently treating it as ordinary business activity risks corrupting her first real financial figures; silently discarding it defeats Q19's whole purpose.
- **`architect` finding:** add an explicit, immutable `Sale.origin: 'genuine' | 'trial'` field, set once at the conversion write, never merchant-editable, never changed afterward — same shape as `SaleItem.pricePaid` (D33), not a mutable capability. Inferring trial status from the seeded Products instead was evaluated and rejected: `Product.active` (Q21) is deliberately general-purpose, not sample-scoped, so a real merchant later deactivating a real product would have her genuine historical sales of it silently misclassified too — a correctness bug worse than the one being solved. Additive field on an existing aggregate, no RFC trigger (same class as D36). The replayed Sale also needs a synthetic wrapping `Session` (schema requires `Sale.sessionId`) — genuinely new, not previously flagged, feeds directly into the eventual Architecture Gap Analysis. Nahui's own internal registration-speed metric (`company/backlog.md` #1) should still count the write regardless of origin — it measures write-path performance, not merchant-facing revenue truth. No second classification needed on `Product`/`InventoryUnit` — Q21 already keeps seeded items off every current selling/reporting surface.
- **`ux-designer` finding:** disclose once, at the conversion moment, that this first sale is now genuinely hers (reusing the disclose-before-the-consequential-moment shape `onboarding.md` §3.4c/§3.6 Variant C already establish, inverted in direction — confirming it's real, not disclaiming it) — never repeated afterward. Recommended against any lasting visible marker, arguing full parity in Results is what actually proves the value loop, and that a permanent badge would reintroduce the "es un ejemplo" framing Q19 exists to eliminate.
- **These two conflict directly, and applying `architect`'s exclusion literally (every `reports.md` aggregation) would leave Results empty right after conversion — recreating the exact problem Q19 was built to avoid.** Resolved by Main, overnight, 2026-09-03, since the Product Owner was unavailable to arbitrate — **flagged explicitly for her review, not a fully confident call:**
  - **Aggregate/summary figures that assert something about her real business performance** — Total histórico, ticket promedio, producto estrella/sales-trend headline statements, Rendimiento por bazar — **filter to `origin = 'genuine'`**. These are exactly the figures where a fabricated first data point does real harm to trust in Nahui's core "real intelligence about her business" premise.
  - **Per-transaction views that only demonstrate the mechanism, not a claim about total business performance** — Historial's session listing, Session/receipt detail ("Por producto" breakdown) — **show the trial Sale as-is, full parity**, satisfying the original goal of letting her explore the complete loop without repeating a sale.
  - `ux-designer`'s one-time disclosure copy/moment is adopted as-is regardless of the above — it's the user-facing acknowledgment; the aggregate/detail split above is a backend correctness question she's unlikely to ever consciously notice.
- **Naming:** `'trial'`, not `'demo'`/`'ejemplo'` — both already reserved for the unrelated "Ver un ejemplo" path (`demo-mode.md`'s own scope note).
- **Named, not resolved:** a low-probability Claim Token edge case (a trial-replay Sale on an already-`paid`-tier conversion would auto-generate a Claim Token like any other Sale, no origin-based exception exists in the domain model) — same class of deferral as Q18, pilot-scale, not a blocker.
- **Status: Superseded, 2026-09-03 (Product Owner).** The Product Owner corrected the underlying premise this entry depended on: the demo/sample experience is explicitly disposable and does *not* need to convert into her real Business/account — no conversion moment exists, so there is no replayed Sale to classify. `Sale.origin`, replay-at-conversion, and the synthetic wrapping Session are all withdrawn, not built. See `product/02-ux/acceso-dm.md` and `decision-log.md` D51 for what actually shipped instead — a disposable, non-convertible entry route reusing the existing "Ver un ejemplo" mechanism, the same class `decision-log.md` D19 already governs. Kept here, unresolved-in-its-original-form but not deleted, per this log's own non-deletion rule.

### Q21 — How does a merchant on the new "Empezar con catálogo de arranque" path remove or replace a pre-seeded sample Product she doesn't want to keep?

- **Question:** Q19's drafted `onboarding.md` amendment (§2.2d) seeds a representative sample Catalog for this path, with a required post-first-sale "Haz que sea tuyo" transition letting her set her real identity and add her own real products. It doesn't address what happens to the *sample* Products already sitting in her Catalog — whether she can delete them, whether they simply coexist indefinitely alongside her real additions, or something else.
- **Why this can't be resolved as a footnote:** verified directly against `inventory.md` in full — no Product-deletion or -replacement mechanism exists anywhere in the current Approved spec. §3.9 "Descartar" only discards an *uncommitted*, in-progress receiving batch, never an already-created Product; §11's future-considerations list doesn't name one either. This is a genuine new capability gap, not an oversight in the Q19 amendment specifically.
- **Not yet resolved:** whether to build Product deletion/archival as a general Inventario capability (benefiting every merchant, not just path-4 entrants), scope it narrowly to sample-Product cleanup only, or leave stale sample rows as an accepted, disclosed cost of the path.
- **Resolution (Product Owner, 2026-08-30):** not deletion — an **active/inactive** state on `Product`. An inactive Product disappears from the selling grid/active Catalog view (so a merchant never sees a stale sample item, or a real item she's discontinued, when trying to sell) but stays fully intact for history, `reports.md`, and every existing `Sale`/`SaleItem` that already references it — no data loss, no broken historical reads. **Scoped as a general Inventario capability, not narrowly to Q19/Q21's sample-cleanup case** — the Product Owner's own framing: this is the normal lifecycle for any merchant retiring a discontinued product, not a Q19-specific fix. Resolves both the sample-data cleanup problem and the general need in one mechanism.
- **Not yet designed:** the actual toggle affordance (where in `inventory.md` a merchant sets a Product active/inactive), whether `reports.md`'s existing figures need any caveat/label change for Sales against now-inactive Products (almost certainly not — historical figures shouldn't change based on a Product's *current* active state), and an `architect` check on whether `active`/`inactive` needs to be a new `Product` field or can derive from something already modeled. Routed to `ux-designer`/`architect`, not designed here.
- **Status:** Resolved in principle (mechanism decided) — build details open, routed to `ux-designer`/`architect`.
- **Amendment, 2026-09-03 — reclassified onto Q19's own critical path, not a side capability.** The Product Owner corrected the relationship: Q21 isn't merely "no longer blocks Q19" — it's the actual mechanism the "Haz que sea tuyo" conversion moment (Q19 §2.2d) depends on. When she converts, the pre-seeded sample Products cannot be deleted (the first demo Sale already references them, and history/`reports.md` must stay intact) — they must be set **inactive**, disappearing from her active selling grid while the Sale record stays whole. Without Q21's active/inactive field, Q19's own conversion step has no way to get the sample catalog out of her way. Treat Q19 + Q20 + Q21 as one coherent value-first onboarding capability, built and reviewed together, not three independently-sequenced items.
- **Correction, 2026-09-03 (Product Owner, later the same day) — the amendment directly above is void.** Q19 no longer has a conversion moment at all (see Q19's own superseding note below) — there is no "Haz que sea tuyo" step for Q21 to be a critical-path dependency of. Q21 reverts to what it was before this amendment: a real, independently-valuable Inventario capability (active/inactive Products for genuine discontinued-product lifecycle), unrelated to Q19's own sequencing, buildable whenever prioritized on its own merits. **Status: Resolved in principle (mechanism decided), build details open — not blocked by, or blocking, Q19/Acceso DM.**

### Q19 — Should a fourth Onboarding path let a DM-qualified merchant reach a completed sale before any setup, using a pre-loaded representative catalog?

- **Question:** Today's real Onboarding paths (Empezar gratis / Activar plan de pago) require, in order: identity capture (`Business.name`) → catalog naming (≥1 Product) → a separate Inventario visit to register stock, before "Iniciar Sesión Rápida" is even reachable (corrected 2026-08-27 — "Venta rápida" is the prototype-only renaming `company/CLAUDE.md`'s terminology-drift rule flags as not yet landed back in the Approved spec; using the current Approved-spec term here instead) — verified precisely against `onboarding.md` and the current React implementation. The Product Owner proposed that merchants entering via the Concierge/DM pilot (`company/merchant-validation-concierge-pilot.md` §0), having already had a real qualifying conversation before touching any link, could instead land on a pre-seeded, real, kept Business with a representative catalog already in place — completing a sale as the very first interaction, with customization explained afterward.
- **Architect finding:** No domain-model blocker. `Sale`/`Product`/`Business`/`InventoryUnit` are structurally agnostic to how their data was created, and the write path this needs (`commitLot()`) already exists and is proven via the existing "Ver un ejemplo" Onboarding path. **But "Ver un ejemplo" is the wrong vehicle for this** — it's explicitly non-convertible/disposable by design (`decision-log.md` D19: "no es tu negocio real, y no vas a poder convertirlo en tu negocio real después"). This proposal wants a real, kept, ongoing Business that starts pre-populated — a genuinely new, fourth path, not a relabeling of the third one. It would need its own spec section, its own name (not "ejemplo," not "demo" — both already reserved for other concepts per `demo-mode.md`'s own scope note), a distinct entry point/link so self-serve traffic is untouched, and a new `decision-log.md` entry.
- **UX finding (`ux-designer`):** The "lead with value" instinct is sound specifically for this population (trust-building already happened in conversation, before touching the link) — not a general case for cold self-serve traffic. Two real tensions named: (1) a pre-filled, not-her-own catalog demonstrates the interaction feels fast, but doesn't on its own validate that *her* actual catalog/inventory friction (`company/CLAUDE.md`'s core thesis) is solved, unless the "make it yours" transition afterward is real and immediate; (2) **Authentication (phone/OTP) must stay in this flow** — even "Ver un ejemplo" sits behind it today, and skipping it for DM-qualified merchants would foreclose ever learning whether phone-entry is a barrier for a population genuinely worth asking, directly undermining the concierge pilot's own future usefulness for that question.
- **Not yet resolved / not built:** whether to invest in this fourth path at all, given the concierge pilot itself is still an unconfirmed diagnostic (`merchant-validation-concierge-pilot.md` §1 non-goal #3 — not a pivot to concierge-style onboarding as Nahui's standing model). **Recommendation on record, not yet actioned:** launch the pilot's two-loop design first using the *existing*, unmodified Onboarding flow (`merchant-validation-concierge-pilot.md` §0), and let Loop 2's own instrumentation show whether DM-qualified, genuinely-interested merchants actually stall in today's onboarding sequence before building this speculatively. If they do, this entry has everything needed to move fast.
- **Status:** Resolved (Product Owner, 2026-08-27) — **build before the pilot launches**, not deferred. The Product Owner's own framing, recorded here rather than paraphrased: this is a structural sequencing problem (today's onboarding delays the first value moment past three real setup stages — identity, catalog, inventory — before a sale is possible), not a pilot-driven optimization; the pilot's own instrumentation stays fully valid under the new sequence (confirmed: the `demo_*` events watch state transitions, not which path reached them), so nothing about launching this first weakens the pilot. See Q20 for a related structural change now bundled into the same spec-amendment work. Next step: `ux-designer` drafts the actual `onboarding.md` amendment (Migration Workflow), once Q20's own architect check returns.

- **Amendment, 2026-08-30 — the flow's own sequencing is reconsidered, not just when identity/catalog capture happen within it.** The Product Owner clarified the proposal isn't "remove OTP" — it's changing *when* Authentication itself runs: `DM → pre-seeded catalog → first completed Sale → "¿quieres empezar tu propio negocio?" → only then, Authentication + real Business creation`. The pre-seeded catalog is explicitly a temporary trial environment, not her real business, until she chooses to convert it — Authentication becomes the actual conversion moment, not an upfront gate before any value is shown.
  - **This changes what the CP1 phone-friction question in the concierge pilot actually measures, named explicitly rather than silently carried over:** the original design (above) would have tested "does phone-entry deter someone *before* she's seen any value." This version tests "does phone-entry deter someone from *converting* a trial she already liked into a real account" — a related but genuinely different signal, arguably more directly useful for adoption (it's closer to the real decision point), but not a like-for-like continuation of the original CP1 framing. `merchant-validation-concierge-pilot.md` §7's PF5 definition needs a matching update once this ships.
  - **New architecture question this reopens, not yet checked:** the original Architect/UX findings above evaluated a version where Authentication still ran first, and only identity/catalog-naming were deferred *after* it. This version requires a merchant to interact with the app — see a catalog, complete a Sale — with **no User, no Business, no session identity established at all**, before any write happens. Whether a `Sale` can meaningfully exist pre-Authentication and then "attach" to a real, newly-authenticated Business at conversion time — versus needing some other mechanism (e.g., the trial state living entirely outside the domain model until conversion, then seeded fresh at that point, the same shape "Ver un ejemplo"'s own seed generation already uses) — has not been checked against `domain-model.md`/`architecture-principles.md`. Routed to `architect`, not resolved here.
  - **Status of this amendment:** direction confirmed by the Product Owner; the specific mechanism (pre-auth trial state vs. seed-at-conversion) is open pending the architect check above. Do not build against the original auth-first sequencing above once this check returns — this amendment supersedes it if the mechanism clears.

- **Amendment, 2026-09-03 — Q19/Q20/Q21 unified as one capability, pre-pilot work, sequencing set explicitly.** The Product Owner corrected the dependency model: Q21's active/inactive mechanism (see that entry's own 2026-09-03 amendment) is not a side capability — it's what lets the conversion moment (§2.2d) actually remove the sample catalog from her active selling experience without deleting Sale-referenced history. Q20 makes the replacement itself efficient (each real Product she adds during/after conversion gets its stock in the same step, not a second Inventario trip). **Sequencing, as directed:** (1) resume/complete the stalled `architect` check on the auth-timing mechanism (the Q19 amendment directly above), now scoped to confirm the *whole* conversion sequence — Authentication + real Business creation + deactivating sample Products (Q21) + adding real Products with initial stock (Q20) — composes cleanly, not just the auth-timing question in isolation; (2) update `onboarding.md`/`inventory.md`/`decision-log.md` consistently as one amendment covering all three; (3) implement in `product/02c-high-fidelity-prototype/`; (4) run the standard review/test cycle. **The Meta campaign does not launch or change until this full sequence closes.**

- **Status: Superseded, 2026-09-03 (Product Owner, later the same day) — this entire "fourth Onboarding path" direction is withdrawn.** The Product Owner challenged the premise directly: the current implementation is entirely local-storage-based, and there is no requirement that the demo experience convert into her real Business/account at all. Under that simpler constraint, a genuinely new, kept, convertible fourth `OnboardingPath` is unnecessary — the existing, already-shipped, disposable "Ver un ejemplo" path (`decision-log.md` D19) already provides the exact loop needed (sample catalog → sale → inventory change → Results), and the only real gap was that it sat behind the same phone/OTP friction a self-serve visitor sees. A further round of scoping (should Authentication itself be skipped, not merely reused) resolved in favor of a small, separate entry route — `product/02-ux/acceso-dm.md`, `decision-log.md` D51 — that composes the existing "Ver un ejemplo" domain writes (unmodified) behind a runtime URL marker with a fixed demo-only credential, never introducing a new `OnboardingPath` value, never touching `onboarding.md`'s three existing paths. This entry, and Q22 above, are superseded by that document; this log's own non-deletion rule keeps them here rather than removing them.

### Q20 — Should Onboarding capture initial inventory quantity at the same moment a Product is created, instead of as a separate Inventario step?

- **Question:** Today, `onboarding.md` §2.2a's "Define lo que vendes" step creates `Product` rows only (name + `defaultPrice`), with zero stock — a merchant must then separately visit Inventario → "Registrar mercancía" to actually have anything sellable (confirmed directly in `store.tsx`'s `createProducts()`, which never writes an `InventoryUnit`). The Product Owner's read: for a small merchant, naming a product and setting how many she has of it are naturally one action, not two — creating a product and then being asked to separately "register merchandise" for the same item feels like performing the same mental task twice. Proposal: capture an initial quantity right at product-creation time in Onboarding, removing that step entirely from first-run setup. Inventario stays exactly as it is today for everything else — later restocks, corrections, recounts, stock movements — this only touches the very first quantity a newly-created Product starts with.
- **Why this needs an Architect check before `ux-designer` writes anything:** merging Product creation and initial `InventoryUnit` creation into a single write is a new pattern — today's two-step design exists as two separate writes (`createProducts()` at Onboarding, `commitLot()` at Inventario), and `ux-designer`'s earlier review of Q19 flagged, without resolving, whether this collides with `decision-log.md` D30's idempotency-key discipline or any other write-path invariant. Not yet checked.
- **Relationship to Q19:** complementary, not overlapping — Q19 is about *pre-seeding* a DM-qualified merchant's first session with representative sample data so she never sees an empty catalog; Q20 is about *shortening* the real, everyone-gets-this catalog-creation step itself, for every merchant regardless of entry channel. Both reduce the same underlying problem (setup delaying the first sale) from different angles, and the Product Owner has asked for both to be evaluated and, if sound, built together as one coordinated `onboarding.md` amendment.
- **Architect finding (2026-08-27):** No domain-model blocker. `commitLot()` already creates Product + Lot + InventoryEntry + InventoryUnit atomically today whenever a brand-new Product name is entered through Inventario — merging Onboarding's product-creation step into that same shape is not a new write pattern. The `decision-log.md` D30 idempotency concern `ux-designer` flagged doesn't hold up: D30 is a key-per-attempt rule, not a key-per-aggregate rule, and Onboarding's product-creation write is already keyed/atomic (`onboarding.md` line 370) — extending its payload doesn't change that guarantee. **One real, previously-unflagged consequence, not a blocker but real scope:** `inventory.md`'s "sin registrar" (never stocked) vs. "0 disponibles" (sold out) Catalog-row distinction (§3.4/§3.5/§3.12/§3.13/§3.17) exists specifically because Onboarding-created Products always start at zero stock today — if Q20 ships, that state becomes unreachable through the primary onboarding path it was built to handle, and needs its own review (most likely retirement or rescoping, not a silent no-op). So "Inventario stays exactly as it is today," as originally proposed, isn't quite accurate — this one passage has a direct dependency on the assumption Q20 removes. Composes cleanly with Q19 (both land on the same underlying `commitLot()`-shaped write, confirmed, no second mechanism needed).
- **Status:** Resolved (Product Owner) — build now, scoped **universally** (every Onboarding path, not just DM-qualified entry). **Correction, 2026-09-03:** no longer bundled with Q19 — Q19's own direction changed (see its superseding note above) and no longer touches Onboarding's product-creation step at all. Q20 remains a real, independent product improvement, buildable on its own schedule whenever prioritized; the flagged `inventory.md` "sin registrar" follow-up (§54 above) still applies whenever it ships.

### Q13 — Should "NFC adoption rate" (% of Sales sold via `nfc` vs. `buttons`) be free-tier or paid-tier?

- **Question:** The Product Owner's 2026-08-04 Medium-Fidelity comprehension pass asked whether Resultados gives merchants enough insight, naming NFC adoption rate as one candidate metric. Unlike the other four metrics raised in the same request (sales trends, top products, ticket average, event performance — all either already Approved in `reports.md` or a direct variant of an already-Approved section), NFC adoption rate has zero precedent anywhere in the current `reports.md` — it isn't mentioned in any of its 14 screen states, so there's no existing tier placement to inherit.
- **Architect finding:** Fully computable now from data that already exists — `Session.operatingMode` (`buttons | nfc`) is a real, stored, per-Session field, resolved once at Session-open (D23) and immutable while `active`. Sale references `sessionId`, so "% of Sales via NFC" = `count(Sales where session.operatingMode = nfc) / count(all Sales)`, a plain join over two already-stored fields — no new field, no new tracking mechanism, no RFC. D25's "never delete historical data" invariant means the metric stays meaningful even after a `subscriptionTier` downgrade (historical `operatingMode` values are untouched; only future Sessions lose `nfc` eligibility). This is purely a **tier-placement** question, the same shape `reports.md` §8 item 7 already flagged and left as "this doc's inference, not an explicit instruction" for "Rendimiento por bazar." Applying `reports.md` §10's own established test (per-Product/aggregate "counts" = free-tier eligible, per the current Approved precedent; venue/customer "segmentation" = paid-tier): NFC adoption rate is arithmetic over Sale counts, the same shape as the free-tier-eligible metrics, but it's also arguably a signal about a *paid capability's* usage (NFC selling itself requires `subscriptionTier=paid`, D27) — a plausible argument either way, not resolved by the existing precedent alone.
- **Not yet resolved:** whether this belongs in the free "counts" tier (consistent with sales trends/top products/ticket average) or the paid tier (since it measures usage of a paid-only capability). Needs a Product Owner call before `ux-designer` commits it to a specific section/gate in `reports.md`.
- **Status:** Open.

### Q9 — Is "venue/bazaar" its own identity, distinct from an Event's freeform Nombre?

- **Question:** "Rendimiento por bazar" (`reports.md` §3.9) could only group by exact string match on `Nombre`, since there was no Venue/Location entity in `domain-model.md`. A typo or renaming across visits could silently fragment one venue into multiple rows.
- **Resolution (Product Owner):** Introduce a lightweight Venue entity now, rather than deferring. Shape: `id`, `businessId`, `displayName`, optional address/notes, `active` status. Event references a Venue instead of relying on freeform exact-name matching. When creating an Event, the merchant selects an existing Venue or creates a new one with minimal friction. Explicitly not a full location-management module.
- **Architect finding:** Venue is a new aggregate root (same independent-identity pattern as Product, D2), owned by Selling (its only consumer is Event — mirrors the existing Supplier/Lot precedent, avoids an unforced new Selling→Inventory dependency). Venue.displayName replaces Event's freeform `Nombre` entirely; Venue's optional address/notes replaces Event's freeform `Lugar` entirely — both retired, not kept alongside Venue. `Event.venueId` is a required reference (not nullable), resolved via an inline create-or-select picker mirroring Inventario's Product picker. Because this is the first new aggregate root since Domain Model v1 froze, and it retires fields in two already-Approved UX specs, Architect routed it through a formal RFC rather than a bare decision-log entry.

### Q2 — Is an untagged InventoryUnit sellable when `registrationMode = nfc`?

- **Question:** If a merchant defers tagging and a customer wants to buy that untagged unit while in `nfc` mode (a scan-only surface, no product grid), there's nothing to scan.
- **Resolution (Product Owner):** Untagged units are never sellable in `nfc` mode — no data-model exception, no mid-session mode switch, and explicitly no manual-fallback picker embedded in the NFC surface (which would functionally recreate Buttons mode inside an NFC session). Instead, the merchant is guided to tag the unit immediately when the situation arises.
- **Architect finding:** Adopted as proposed — this is the principle-preserving option (equivalent to the Architect's original option (a)), and correctly avoids reintroducing a second selling surface (`architecture-principles.md` #1, `vision.md`'s mode-switching prohibition). The redirect from Selling into Inventario's Asignar Tags flow is a sanctioned UI hand-off pattern already used elsewhere (`events.md`'s "Ver resumen en Resultados," `home.md`'s upcoming-Event card) — not a new architectural exception.
- **Two items remain, deliberately not resolved here:**
  1. **UX design gap (not a decision, a design task):** Home's `nfc` selling surface (`home.md` §3.10) has no product grid or picker at all — there's currently no affordance through which "attempting to sell an untagged unit" could surface as a discrete event. Whoever designs this must not let the redirect resolve into an enumerable, selectable product list (that would recreate the rejected fallback picker). Flagged for `ux-designer`.
  2. **"Emergency Sale" is explicitly deferred as a future RFC**, not decided now. It requires reopening `decision-log.md` D10: if an emergency-sold unit is tagged *after* the sale, that tag's first-ever scan would be misread as a customer loyalty claim under D10's current status-based disambiguation (`sold` = claim scan), not as the deferred sale confirmation. Any future RFC for Emergency Sale must resolve this collision explicitly, not just the Session-close-gating and traceability points already named.
- **Status:** Resolved (principle) — two follow-ups tracked above, not open questions requiring further Product Owner input.

### Q3 — Tie-break rule for two simultaneously active Events

- **Question:** Home's resolution logic assumes at most one Event is ever `active` at a time. Nothing in the Foundation prevented a Business from having two Events with overlapping date ranges both active simultaneously.
- **Resolution (Product Owner):** Rather than add tie-break logic, remove the ambiguous state entirely — a Business may not create or activate an Event whose date range overlaps an already-scheduled-or-active Event. A deliberate MVP simplification; concurrent Events belong to a future multi-employee capability, not built now.
- **Architect finding:** Additive, no RFC needed — a same-context, same-entity uniqueness validation, not a cross-bounded-context concern. Fully eliminates the need for any tie-break logic in `home.md` by construction; no change to `home.md` required. `events.md`'s "Nuevo Evento" flow will need a new blocked-save/validation error state for the overlap check (a UX design task, not resolved here). Flagged one framing nuance: the "future multi-employee capability" this is deferred to is an *intra-tenant* multi-user problem (multiple sellers under one Business), structurally different from backlog #3's *cross-tenant* bazaar-recommendation feature — coherent as a scoping choice, but not blocked on a data source the same way backlog #3 is; it could be built whenever prioritized.
- **Applied:** `domain-model.md`'s Event description now states this invariant; `decision-log.md` D17 records the decision and reasoning.
- **Status:** Resolved.
