# 0014 — `Invitation.targetHint` becomes required and enforced at acceptance

Status: **Accepted** (Product Owner, 2026-09-15 — "I think email is fine," plus her own refinement of the enforcement mechanism, incorporated below). `architect`-drafted at Main's request, following a live Product Owner security question during production testing (`product-decisions.md` Q30).

Supersedes `product/99-rfc/0013-invitation-token-based.md` §1's ruling on `targetHint` specifically ("real, useful, explicitly non-canonical... never matched against, and never promoted to, the accepting `User`'s identity") — on that one point only. The rest of RFC 0013 (token as `Invitation`'s canonical/discoverable identity, the two-write CAS acceptance sequencing, expiry, single-use-by-construction, the decision to reject a channel-type-picker on `Invitation` itself) is untouched and stays fully in force.

## The idea

`Invitation`'s own discoverability mechanism doesn't change: the token, embedded in a shareable link, stays the sole way to find and open the offer, regardless of delivery channel — RFC 0013's central point, unrelitigated here. What changes is a second, independent gate at the moment of acceptance: `targetHint` (today, an OWNER-entered email address) becomes **required** at `create_invitation()` — no more optional field.

**Enforcement mechanism, refined from the initial draft per the Product Owner's own two suggestions ("an additional step asking [if] the email... match[es], continue if not rejection" or "the invitation just be signing with email"):** rather than letting the invited person authenticate via *any* method (phone/Google/email) and checking for a match afterward, the Invitation-acceptance flow routes directly into the **Email** authentication method specifically, with the email field pre-filled and locked to `targetHint`'s value — she verifies that exact address, or she doesn't get in. This isn't merely the simpler of her two options — it's the *only* one that actually works cleanly against the current schema: `AuthIdentity(type='google').identifier` is the provider's opaque subject ID, "deliberately not the OAuth-provider email" (`domain-model.md`'s own `AuthIdentity` entry) — Nahui never captures a Google sign-in's real email address anywhere. A post-hoc "check if any of her verified identities' email matches" (the initial draft's mechanism) would therefore always fail for a Google-only sign-in even when her actual Google account email is the exact right one, which isn't a real security improvement, just a confusing false rejection. Locking the acceptance flow to Email specifically sidesteps this entirely — no cross-type matching, no ambiguity, and it matches what the Product Owner asked for more directly.

This directly answers the security gap the Product Owner raised live: today, a leaked or forwarded invite link lets any stranger who can complete any supported auth method join her Business as a SELLER, regardless of who the OWNER actually meant to invite. RFC 0013 §4 named this exact risk explicitly and accepted it as a known, low-probability risk at pilot scale ("zero live instances"); the Product Owner's live report tonight is new information changing that calculus, not a reason to relitigate the original ruling.

**Phone is deliberately excluded from this mechanism.** `decision-log.md` D63 records the Product Owner's own explicit instruction: *"phone number must not be the canonical identity of either the invitation or `BusinessMembership`"* and *"it would be inconsistent for an OWNER to need the SELLER's phone number in order to invite them."* Binding acceptance to phone — in any form — would directly contradict an instruction she gave this same day, for the same feature. Email, by contrast, she explicitly reserved room for in that same instruction: *"An email address used to deliver or target an invitation may be useful for security/UX, but please do not assume it must become the canonical identity of the resulting membership."* This RFC uses email as exactly that — a security/UX gate on *acceptance*, never as `BusinessMembership`'s own canonical identity (which stays `userId`, unchanged).

## What it touches

- `domain-model.md` / `ubiquitous-language.md`: `Invitation.targetHint`'s entry — from "optional, advisory-only, structurally never matched" to "required; the acceptance flow authenticates specifically through it." No schema addition — the field already exists (RFC 0013).
- `accept_invitation()` (real SQL RPC, `product/02c-high-fidelity-prototype/supabase/migrations/`) — gains a defensive precondition check, before the existing status CAS, so a mismatched attempt never consumes the token: the authenticating user's resolved `AuthIdentity(type='email', verifiedAt is not null)` must include a row matching `lower(trim(targetHint.value))`. Enforced server-side regardless of what the client UI offers — never trust the client alone to have locked the auth method correctly. On mismatch, raises a new, distinct error (`invitation_identity_mismatch`), never `invitation_not_available` — the invitation itself is still perfectly valid, just not for this person/account.
- `create_invitation()` — `p_target_hint` changes from `default null` (optional) to required; reject a null value going forward.
- `regenerate_invitation()` — no change; it never touches `target_hint`.
- `settings.md` §2.7/§3.12 ("Invitar a alguien") — email becomes a required field on the invite-creation form, not optional. New copy needed explaining why (a short, honest line — "para que solo esa persona pueda aceptar").
- `authentication.md` §2.0/§2.2a/§3.9a-§3.13a (Invitation-acceptance flow) — the biggest design change: for an Invitation entry point specifically, the method-picker (§3.2a "Elegir cómo entrar") is skipped entirely in favor of a direct, pre-filled Email-verification sub-flow. Two new states needed: (1) **already-authenticated-under-the-wrong-account** — the device already has a valid session (RFC 0013 step 4's existing fast path), but that User's own verified email doesn't match `targetHint`; can't silently proceed (would violate the whole point of this RFC) and can't silently fail either — needs a real "this invitation is for a different email — sign out and try with the right one?" state, not routed into the generic `invitation_not_available` dead end. (2) **mismatch after a fresh Email-verification attempt** — narrower than it sounds, since the field is pre-filled/locked, but she could still complete OTP/link verification for an *existing* account under that email if she already has one linked elsewhere with a different primary identity — treat identically to (1).
- **Not touched:** `Invitation.token`'s own role as the discoverability mechanism, the two-write CAS sequencing, expiry, single-use-by-construction, `BusinessMembership`'s schema, `EventAssignment`, the Owner-creation invariant, `AuthIdentity`'s own two invariants, and the general (non-Invitation) "Elegir cómo entrar" method-picker — this RFC only narrows the *Invitation-specific* entry point, not first-time sign-up generally.

## Why

**The security gap is real and freshly confirmed, not hypothetical.** RFC 0013 §4's own risk acceptance was reasoned from "pilot scale, zero live instances" — a probabilistic argument that gets weaker, not stronger, as real usage grows, and the Product Owner is now actively thinking about exactly this exposure while testing the live, real feature. Closing it costs nothing structural: the token stays the universal discovery mechanism RFC 0013 designed; this is a narrower, additive acceptance-time check layered on top, not a redesign.

**Why this specific mechanism doesn't reverse RFC 0013's actual point.** RFC 0013 §1's central finding was that `Invitation`'s *identity* must not be a communication channel — a type-picker on the Invitation itself would force the invited person to authenticate through whichever channel the OWNER happened to guess, which is backwards. This RFC doesn't touch that: the token remains sole and canonical for *finding* the offer. What's added is a second, independent check at the moment of *accepting* it — closer in spirit to RFC 0013 §4's own already-named, already-considered-but-undesigned mitigation ("a post-acceptance soft warning if the authenticated identity's own email doesn't match `targetHint`") than to a reversal. This RFC goes further than that soft-warning idea (a hard block, not just a warning) because a warning alone doesn't actually close the security gap the Product Owner is asking about — it only surfaces it after the fact, by which point the wrong person already has SELLER access.

**The tradeoff, accepted explicitly by the Product Owner, 2026-09-15 ("I think email is fine").** This is not a free win: it reintroduces real friction. The OWNER must know the SELLER's correct email in advance and enter it accurately — the exact category of friction RFC 0013 existed to remove for phone specifically (RFC 0013's whole premise was decoupling from needing to know a channel in advance). It's a smaller cost than the retired phone-keyed design (email carries none of the Mexican phone-verification/WhatsApp Business Sender friction that motivated D62/D63's move away from phone), but it is not zero — accepted anyway, in favor of closing the leaked-link exposure.

## Mismatch UX, named explicitly rather than left to a builder to invent

Locking the acceptance flow to a pre-filled Email field (per the refined mechanism above) prevents the common typo-at-acceptance-time case by construction — she isn't typing a different email by accident, the field is already filled with what the OWNER entered. What's left, narrower than the initial draft's version of this problem: (1) the OWNER herself typo'd or entered the wrong email at invite-creation time (a real, still-possible case — the fix is the same either way: she can correct `targetHint` on the still-`pending` row via an ordinary `UPDATE`, the existing OWNER-scoped `invitations_update` RLS policy already permits this, no new RPC needed — or regenerate the invitation entirely); (2) the invited person already has an existing Nahui session on this device under a *different* identity than `targetHint` (RFC 0013 step 4's existing-session fast path) — needs a clear, non-alarming "this invitation is for a different email" state with a way to sign out and try again, not a silent proceed (defeats this RFC's whole point) or a silent fail (looks like a broken link). Neither state should ever say "this invitation is invalid" — that's false; the invitation is fine, it's the wrong person/account for it. Designing these two screens is `ux-designer`'s job, not fixed here — named so they aren't silently dropped as a "just handle the error" afterthought.

## Backward compatibility

Already-`accepted` Invitations (including any accepted tonight) are completely untouched — the new check only runs inside the live acceptance CAS path, never retroactively against history. Any `Invitation` still `pending` with `targetHint = null` at the moment this ships (created before enforcement) is treated as a legacy row exempt from the check — enforcement is null-guarded, not a hard-fail-everything migration. No backfill needed; the OWNER can optionally set a hint on an old pending row to opt it in, or simply let it expire/regenerate normally.

## RFC-trigger test

Reverses a specific, explicitly-stated structural guarantee inside an Accepted RFC (0013 §1: "never required, never treated as identity... structurally never matched against the accepting User's identity") and redefines what `targetHint` *means* in `ubiquitous-language.md` (from a non-canonical delivery aid to an enforced identity precondition) — the same weight class RFC 0013 itself was for RFC 0012 §3. **RFC required**, following the exact template RFC 0013 already used against RFC 0012: a new, numbered RFC that narrowly supersedes one specific ruling in an existing Accepted RFC, leaving everything else in that RFC untouched.

## Resolution

**Accepted, 2026-09-15.** The Product Owner confirmed email as the mechanism and proposed the two enforcement shapes weighed in "The idea" above; the email-locked-authentication shape was chosen over post-hoc any-method matching for the concrete technical reason given there (Google sign-in never captures a real email `AuthIdentity`). Promoted to `decision-log.md` D70. Routed to `ux-designer` for the two new authentication.md states and `settings.md`'s invite-form change.
