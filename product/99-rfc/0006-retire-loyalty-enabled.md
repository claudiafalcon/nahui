# 0006 — Retire `loyaltyEnabled`; Frequent Customers as a Paid-tier-only capability, entitlement not merchant toggle

Status: Accepted — promoted to `decision-log.md` D40

**Revision note:** an earlier internal draft of this RFC proposed unconditional Claim Token generation for every Business regardless of tier, reasoning that Free-tier merchants should accumulate loyalty history before upgrading. The Product Owner explicitly rejected that clause. This version replaces it in full — Free tier does not participate in Frequent Customers at all, by design.

## The idea

`loyaltyEnabled` is retired outright — as a Business Capability and as a ubiquitous-language term. Not narrowed, not renamed, not kept dormant: deleted. Frequent Customers — customer recognition, loyalty participation, reward progress, customer-level purchase/spend history, reward confirmation, and the customer intelligence derived from it — is a **single, Paid-tier-only capability**, entitled purely by `subscriptionTier`, never by merchant configuration.

- **Free tier: Frequent Customers is entirely unavailable.** No Claim Token generation. No NFC claim eligibility (already correctly excluded — `nfc ∈ registrationMode` already requires `subscriptionTier = paid`, D27). No QR offered. No customer-registration surface reachable. No loyalty participation, no reward progress, no customer-level purchase/spend history, no reward confirmation, no derived customer intelligence. Nothing invisible or partially built for Free — this is deliberate MVP scope, not an oversight.
- **Paid tier: Frequent Customers becomes available automatically**, as a consequence of `subscriptionTier = paid` — Ana does not enable it, does not disable it, and there is no Business-level field, screen, or action anywhere in the product that lets her try. `registrationMode` still determines *which* mechanism a given Paid-tier Sale can use (NFC scan vs. Claim Token/QR), never *whether* Frequent Customers exists for that Business.
- **Claim resolution unifies onto one gate.** Both Claim mechanisms — NFC tag scan (already tier-derived via `registrationMode`, D27) and Sale-level Claim Token (previously gated by `loyaltyEnabled`, now gated directly on `subscriptionTier = paid`, generated at every Paid-tier Sale's finalization regardless of `registrationMode`) — read as one capability, present or absent per tier, not two independently-gated mechanisms plus a merchant switch.
- **Per-Sale behavior is separate and stays exactly as previously designed.** Once available (Paid tier), Ana still controls, per Sale, whether she actually offers/displays the already-generated Claim Token/QR to that customer — an operational, transient choice at the Digital Receipt moment (`home.md` §3.8f), never a Business-level field, never a change to Business configuration, never something that disables the capability for future Sales.
- **No dormant or partial artifact is introduced for Free-tier Sales.** A Free-tier Sale generates no Claim Token — not even an unused/dormant one. Consistent with `decision-log.md` D26's precedent against speculative pre-modeling ("the one sanctioned dormant-field exception [Supplier]... not a general license to pre-build every eventually-planned field") and `architecture-principles.md` #5.

## Why

The Product Owner's authoritative model, verbatim: *"Frequent Customers is a PAID-tier capability. Free tier: Frequent Customers is unavailable... Do not build an invisible or partially usable Frequent Customers experience for Free as part of the MVP. Paid tier: Frequent Customers becomes available automatically because the merchant's `subscriptionTier` includes it. Ana does NOT enable it. Ana does NOT disable it... Entitlement is derived from `subscriptionTier`, not from merchant configuration."* Commercial framing, for why this sits at the tier boundary rather than being universally available: *"Free = operate the business. Paid = operate the business + understand and grow customer relationships. Frequent Customers belongs to Paid."*

This also directly corrects an earlier internal draft's error, worth naming so it isn't repeated: that draft reasoned that unconditional generation served the same goal D34 originally cited for keeping `loyaltyEnabled` independent of tier (letting Free merchants "start collecting Claims before upgrading"). That goal is explicitly not one this MVP holds — the Product Owner's correction states plainly that Free does not participate in Frequent Customers "at all," full stop, not partially, not invisibly. The error was treating "avoid a toggle" and "make history-building tier-independent" as the same fix; they aren't. The toggle is what's being removed; tier-gating stays, and in fact becomes the *sole* gate, doing double duty as both entitlement and the thing that replaces the toggle.

No `knowledge-mentor` consultation needed — this reuses an already-established pattern (D27's tier-derivation) rather than introducing a new one.

- **D22 introduced `loyaltyEnabled` as "one business capability"** gating whether Claims are collected — a merchant decision, by its own framing. That framing is what's superseded. Nothing else about D22 changes: Claim still resolves through multiple mechanisms (NFC scan, Sale-level Claim Token) converging on the identical terminal write; the Claim Token is still opaque/signed, never the raw Sale ID; Customer Identity still belongs exclusively to the Loyalty platform; the Merchant Application still consumes only Derived Customer Intelligence; both bounded-context read edges (Loyalty-claim→Selling, Intelligence→Loyalty-claim) are unchanged.
- **D25/Q5 formalized `loyaltyEnabled` as bidirectionally merchant-self-service** — this is now moot, since there's no field left to toggle. `subscriptionTier`'s own self-service treatment (bidirectional, pending-value/effective-date pair, never-delete-historical-data invariant) is unaffected.
- **D27 narrowed the self-service list to "exactly two capabilities."** Narrows further, to one — everything else D27 established (NFC as a pure derivation of `subscriptionTier`, `defaultSellingMode` becoming self-service, the welcome-tag-package fulfillment-only framing) is untouched.
- **D29 added an `acknowledged` boolean to both `subscriptionTier` and `loyaltyEnabled`'s pending-value pairs.** The `loyaltyEnabled` half is moot; `subscriptionTier`'s `acknowledged` field, and the entirely-unrelated `nfcAvailabilityNudgeShown` field D29 also introduced, are both untouched.
- **D34 is the entry most directly in tension with this correction, and needs to be read in two halves.** Its correction of D22's *joint visibility gate* (Customer Segmentation/Loyalty Participation Record visible on `subscriptionTier=paid` alone, never a second toggle) was already right and is exactly the shape of reasoning the Product Owner's new correction extends — that half survives untouched. Its *retention and defense of `loyaltyEnabled` itself* — quoted directly because the reasoning, not just the wording, is what's being superseded: *"a deliberate, reasoned independent axis, not an oversight"*, kept specifically so "a Free-tier merchant [could] start collecting Claims before upgrading" — is superseded by this RFC. The corrected model achieves the stated goal of that clause (removing a merchant-facing switch) by removing Free-tier participation entirely, not by making Claim collection universal.

This is a straightforward instance of the "entitlement over configuration" principle this Foundation already applies wherever a merchant decision was found to be unnecessary (D5, D27, D33): a thing that was modeled as a choice is, on inspection, not actually a choice anyone should have to make — it's a consequence of the plan the merchant is already on.
