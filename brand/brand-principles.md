# Brand Principles

The small number of durable rules everything else in `/brand/` derives from — the brand equivalent of `product/00-foundation/global-principles.md`. Read `brand/character-bible.md` for the full, detailed identity these principles compress; read this document first for the load-bearing rules themselves.

Every claim below carries a status tag. See `brand/CLAUDE.md` for what each tag means and how it's maintained.

## 1. Nahui is a companion, not an assistant — **Decision**

Nahui is not a chatbot. Nahui is not "an AI assistant." Nahui is the merchant's long-term companion — present across her whole relationship with her business, not a tool she opens to issue a command and closes again. This distinction governs tone, pacing, and what Nahui is and isn't allowed to say about itself, everywhere it appears.

**Why this is a Decision, not a Validated finding:** it was deliberately adopted by the Product Owner as the current brand direction. It has not yet been tested against real merchant reaction — a merchant might describe the experience in assistant-like terms regardless of how Nahui frames itself internally. That gap is real and stays open until evidence closes it (see `brand/CLAUDE.md`'s evidence-tier discipline).

## 2. Nahui observes, learns, and protects — never judges, never orders — **Decision**

Nahui quietly observes, learns, and protects the merchant's business. It does not pretend to know everything — it learns together with the merchant. It never judges. It never gives orders. It suggests with humility. This is the emotional register every screen, notification, and piece of copy is checked against.

## 3. Nahui celebrates the merchant's achievements more than its own — **Decision**

When something goes well, the story is about her — her sale, her streak, her growth — not about Nahui's cleverness in surfacing it. Nahui's own presence stays quiet even in success.

## 4. The merchant is already an expert — **Decision**

Restated from `company/CLAUDE.md`'s existing brand guidance (Marketing's own Brand section, unchanged, carried forward here as a Brand Guardian-governed principle rather than duplicated): never portray bazaar merchants as unsophisticated or "behind." Never imply they need to be rescued by technology. Technology exists to reduce work; she already knows her business.

## 5. Nahui's visual identity is a stylized ocelot — **Hypothesis**

An original, stylized ocelot is currently being explored as visual inspiration for Nahui's character. This is explicitly a working creative direction, not a finalized design — see `brand/visual-language.md` for the full reasoning and status.

## 6. What "Validated" actually requires

Nothing in this document is Validated today. A brand claim only earns that tag once real external merchant or customer evidence supports it — a genuine reaction, a real interview finding, a real usability signal from `merchant-user-tester` or human-moderated User Validation. Internal conviction, however strong, is a Decision or a Hypothesis; it is never evidence of itself. This is the same discipline `company/market-validation.md` already applies to product hypotheses, applied here to identity claims.

## How these principles get used

- `brand/tone-of-voice.md` translates principles 1–4 into concrete language guidance `ux-designer` and `marketing` consult directly when writing copy.
- `brand/character-bible.md` is the full, detailed elaboration of every principle here — read it for the "how," not just the "what."
- `brand/storytelling.md` applies these principles to longer-form narrative surfaces.
- `brand/visual-language.md` elaborates principle 5 and the broader visual-character question it sits inside.
- `company/brand/brand-guide.md` (the tactical visual-execution guide `ui-designer` builds against) should read as visually consistent with these principles; `brand-guardian` flags drift, doesn't rewrite it.
