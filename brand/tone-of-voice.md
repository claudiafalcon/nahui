# Tone of Voice

Concrete language guidance — what `brand/character-bible.md`'s Voice section (Decision) sounds like in actual copy. This is what `ux-designer` and `marketing` consult directly when drafting merchant- or customer-facing text; `brand/brand-principles.md` states the "why," this document states the "how it reads."

Every claim below is a **Decision** unless marked otherwise — a direct, deliberate translation of already-adopted character-bible principles into language rules, not a new creative direction awaiting testing.

## The register

Direct, warm, plain. Business language before technical language — this document sits alongside, and never overrides, `product/00-foundation/global-principles.md`'s existing structural rule ("business language before technical language," natural Mexican Spanish, never a literal translation from English for anything merchant-facing). What this document adds is the *feeling* of that language: warm without being cute, direct without being blunt, honest without being clinical.

## Learner before advisor — the register that governs every recommendation

Direct expression of `character-bible.md`'s "Earning the right to advise" (Decision): Nahui should never sound like an expert, a narrator, a bank, a fintech, or a virtual assistant. It arrives knowing nothing about a given merchant, and learns from her before it ever gives advice — only after it has actually earned an understanding of *her specific* business does recommendation-shaped language belong in its copy at all.

- **Observation before instruction.** *"He notado algo..."* ("I've noticed something...") is the shape recommendation copy should take once Nahui does have something worth saying — never *"Deberías hacer esto"* ("You should do this"). The first offers something she can weigh; the second assumes a standing to instruct her that hasn't been earned. This is the single clearest test to run any recommendation-shaped copy against.
- **No prescriptive language without a real, merchant-specific basis behind it.** Words like "deberías," "te recomendamos," "lo correcto es" only belong in copy once there's an actual observation of *this* merchant's own data or behavior underneath them — a pattern in her own sales, a choice she's already made. Absent that basis (a brand-new merchant, a feature with no history to draw on yet), default to plain, neutral statement of fact, or to no comment at all, rather than manufacturing advice-shaped language to fill the space.
- **No expert/narrator/bank/fintech/assistant register, regardless of surface.** This applies as much to a notification or a report as to conversational copy: a confident, explaining tone that reads as "here is what you should know" rather than "here is what I noticed" fails this rule even where no literal imperative verb appears.
- **Age is not the axis.** A voice does not have to sound old, formal, or "wise" to violate this — a young, casual, confident "explainer" tone can claim exactly the same unearned authority a stiff corporate one does. What's being tested is whether the copy sounds like it already knows, not how old it sounds.

## What "never gives orders" governs — and what it doesn't — **Decision**, added 2026-10-07

`character-bible.md`'s "never gives orders — no imperative 'you should,' no urgency it hasn't earned" (Decision) is about **standing**, not about **grammar**. It was being read too literally: taken as a ban on the imperative mood as such, it would flag every button on every surface Nahui has ever shipped, including the imperative action labels throughout Approved `product/02-ux/` copy ("Registrar venta," "Continuar," "Cerrar jornada de venta," "No, gracias") and every CTA on `landing/index.html`. That reading is wrong, and this section draws the line explicitly so the rule isn't misapplied on a future review pass.

**The line.** The rule governs **advice- and recommendation-shaped copy** — any sentence in which Nahui expresses a view about what the merchant should do with her own business. It does not govern **control labels, CTAs, section headings, and headlines**, which name a function the merchant is choosing to invoke.

**Why the distinction is real and not a convenience.** A button label is not Nahui speaking to her; it is the name of the thing *she* is about to do. "Registrar venta" on a button is the merchant's own intent, written down so she can tap it — the grammatical imperative there is a label, not an instruction, exactly the way a drawer is labeled rather than commanded. "Deberías registrar tus ventas más seguido" is Nahui forming an opinion about how she runs her business and handing it to her unasked. Those two sentences share a verb mood and share nothing else: one reduces work (`character-bible.md`'s "How Nahui helps"), the other claims a standing Nahui has not earned ("Earning the right to advise"). Collapsing them would make the rule unenforceable, and — worse — would push copy toward limp, hedged control labels ("Quizás quieras registrar una venta") that are both harder to use and *more* patronizing than the plain imperative, failing `global-principles.md`'s "the best interface is the one that stays out of the merchant's way" and Value #4 at the same time.

**In the carve-out — the imperative is correct and preferred:**
- Button and control labels naming an action the merchant initiates: "Registrar venta," "Continuar," "Agregar producto," "Cerrar jornada de venta."
- Decline/escape affordances, which must stay as plain and prominent as the primary action: "No, gracias."
- Navigation, tabs, section headings: "Inventario," "Resultados," "Eventos."
- Marketing CTAs and headlines naming what the product does or what the visitor can do next: "Empezar gratis," "Prueba la demo," "Registra cada venta." *Corrected forward 2026-10-08: this bullet's third example previously read "Registra cada venta en segundos." The grammatical point it illustrates was and remains correct, but the example itself carries an unmeasured performance claim — `landing/BRIEF.md` §4 records that **no latency measurement exists anywhere in the repository** and that `company/backlog.md` #1's own `<3 s` bar is still unmet — and an example in this document gets copied as a pattern. Swapped for the neutral version rather than left standing. Whether "en segundos" may be claimed on any particular surface is a claim-substantiation question for the Product Owner and `marketing`, not a register question, and is not decided here.*
- Instructional microcopy inside a task the merchant has already chosen to start — the step-by-step of a flow she opted into ("Acerca la tag al teléfono"), which is orientation inside her own chosen task, not unsolicited advice about her business.

**Not in the carve-out — the rule applies in full:**
- Anything expressing a view on how she should run, price, stock, or grow her business: "Deberías activar esto," "Te conviene surtir más," "Lo correcto es cerrar la jornada cada día."
- Anything implying she has missed a step or fallen behind: "No olvides...," "Aún no has...," "Recuerda que deberías..."
- Manufactured urgency of any kind, in any grammatical mood — the separate "never use urgency Nahui hasn't earned" rule below stands independently and is not softened by this carve-out. "¡Aprovecha hoy!" is a CTA in form and a violation in substance.
- A control label that smuggles in a judgment rather than naming a function: a button reading "Arreglar mi inventario" is not in the carve-out, because "arreglar" asserts something was wrong.

**The test, for a reviewer.** Ask: *if the merchant read this and ignored it, would Nahui have had an opinion about her that she just declined?* If yes, it's advice-shaped and the rule applies. If there was no opinion to decline — only a function she didn't invoke — it's a control label and the carve-out holds. A finding raised against an imperative that passes this test is not a Blocker and not a Major; it isn't a finding at all.

## Gendered register in Spanish copy — **open gap, not decided here**, added 2026-10-07

**The gap.** No `/brand/` document has ever addressed how Nahui handles grammatical gender in Spanish copy, and the question is unavoidable in Spanish in a way it simply isn't in English — the language forces a choice on almost every noun and adjective referring to a person. `product/00-foundation/global-principles.md`'s Product Language rules cover *which* language and *whose* vocabulary, not this. The gap went unnoticed for as long as it did because in-product copy mostly addresses the merchant directly in second person ("tu negocio," "ya quedaste registrada"), where the choice is either invisible or already settled by who's reading. The first surface to need an actual rule is `landing/index.html`, which speaks *about* an audience rather than *to* one person.

**What that surface currently does — observed, not endorsed.** Three different patterns coexist on the same page:
- **Inclusive masculine for the audience:** "Para vendedores de bazar" (`hero.eyebrow`), "cuando abramos a más vendedores" (`band.lead`), "La app para vendedores de bazar en México" (`og:description`).
- **Feminine for evidence and story:** "nuestra primera entrevista con una vendedora," "Nahui nació platicando con una vendedora...," "Ella conoce su negocio de memoria," "aprendiendo con vendedoras reales," "cada paso lo probamos con vendedoras reales."
- **An explicit doublet in one place:** "la app para vendedoras y vendedores de bazar" (`meta description`).

Read charitably this is close to defensible — the feminine is factually correct wherever it refers to Ana and to the real women actually being interviewed, and the inclusive masculine is the conventional Mexican Spanish default for an open audience. Read less charitably, a visitor who notices will see the page address men and evidence women, and the one doublet makes it clear the question was felt but never settled.

**Why `brand-guardian` is not deciding this.** A gender-register rule for all merchant-facing Spanish would govern every current and future surface, in-product and external, and carries real positioning weight (who Nahui is visibly for) alongside the voice weight. That makes it a **Product Decision**, not a brand-execution call delegated to this document — routed to the Product Owner per `decision-ownership-classification`. The options below are drafted to make her call easier, not to pre-empt it. **None of them is adopted. Until she decides, existing copy is not a violation of this document**, and no Brand Consistency Review may raise a finding against gendered register on the basis of this section.

**Still open as of 2026-10-08, and now load-bearing on a second surface.** The public presentation page (`landing/BRIEF.md`) speaks *about* merchants in the third person throughout — the exact construction this gap governs — so it will have to pick something. It picks it without a rule, and that choice is still not a finding. What *is* already settled and does apply there: where copy refers to real, specific people (the pilot merchants, the vendor actually interviewed), natural gender is simply correct, per "What holds regardless" below.

**Options, drafted neutrally.**

1. **Feminine as the default register throughout.** "Para vendedoras de bazar." Reflects the actual composition of the bazaar segment, makes Ana visible as the person the product was built with, and is the strongest positioning statement of the four. Cost: grammatically excludes male bazaar vendors, who do exist in the segment, and reads as a deliberate statement — which is either the point or a liability depending on the Product Owner's intent.
2. **Inclusive masculine as the default register throughout** (the conventional Mexican Spanish default, and what most of the current page already does). Lowest-friction, invisible to most readers, matches how merchants themselves commonly speak. Cost: the exact asymmetry visible on the page today — a brand whose entire evidence base and pilot user are women addressing its audience in the masculine; silently forgoes a positioning advantage that is factually earned.
3. **Doublets where the audience is plural or indefinite** ("vendedoras y vendedores"), natural gender where a real person is referred to. Explicitly inclusive, already present once on the page, defensible without being a statement. Cost: longer, heavier in headlines and tight UI space, and in merchant-facing Spanish it can read slightly institutional — brushing against this document's own "never corporate" register rule, which is a genuine brand concern and worth weighing as one.
4. **Avoid the construction where possible, natural gender where unavoidable.** Rewrite around the noun — "Para quien vende en bazares," "Para tu negocio de bazar" — rather than choosing a gender at all. Often produces the warmest and most merchant-vocabulary-native line of the four, and sidesteps the issue entirely in most headline positions. Cost: not always available, so it needs one of options 1-3 as the fallback rule anyway; and overused it can read evasive.

**What holds regardless of which option is chosen** — these are not open, and follow directly from existing Decisions:
- Where copy refers to a **real, specific person** (Ana, a real interviewee, a named merchant), natural gender is simply correct. "Una vendedora" describing a real woman is accurate, not a register choice, and no option above changes it.
- Whatever is chosen gets applied **consistently across in-product and external surfaces at once** — the whole purpose of this document is that the acquisition voice and the in-product voice are the same Nahui. A rule adopted for the landing page and not for the app would itself be a brand inconsistency.
- No option licenses `@`/`x` orthography ("vendedor@s," "vendedorxs") in merchant-facing copy. Out of scope of the four options above and inconsistent with `global-principles.md`'s "write how a Mexican bazaar vendor actually talks."
- Whatever is chosen, it is a **Decision**, not Validated — no merchant has been asked how any of these registers reads to her. Testable cheaply at the first real merchant contact.

## Concrete rules

- **State facts before offering an opinion.** "No pudimos guardar. Sigue aquí, intenta de nuevo" — what happened, then what to do — never an apology-first, fact-second structure.
- **Never use urgency Nahui hasn't earned.** No "¡Solo por hoy!," no manufactured scarcity, no countdown language — this is a companion, not a sales funnel. Directly enforces `character-bible.md`'s "never gives orders." *Note (2026-10-07):* this rule is not narrowed by the CTA carve-out above — manufactured urgency fails regardless of whether it appears in a button, a headline, or a sentence.
- **Suggestions read as offers, not instructions.** "Puedes revisar esto en Configuración" (an actual `reports.md` §3.13 precedent), never "Deberías activar esto" or any imperative construction implying she's missed a step. *Scope clarified 2026-10-07:* "any imperative construction" here means within advice-shaped copy — see "What 'never gives orders' governs," above, for why a control label naming a function she chose is not what this rule is about.
- **Celebration is about her, plainly stated, never inflated.** A completed sale, a reached milestone — state what happened in her terms ("Ya quedaste registrada con Ropa Ana"), never dress it up with exclamation-heavy congratulatory copy that centers the platform's cleverness. *Worth naming precisely, since it's since been reused elsewhere:* this exact sentence originates in `product/02-ux-loyalty/customer-loyalty-registration.md` — a **customer's** confirmation of joining a business's loyalty relationship, a passive, low-stakes "you're now recognized" moment. The sentence *shape* (plain, unadorned, her-achievement-first) generalizes cleanly to other registration/milestone moments; the specific verb ("quedaste registrada," you got registered/listed) carries a passive connotation worth double-checking on reuse — see `authentication.md` §3.10c's SELLER-invitation-acceptance reuse of this exact line, flagged in this document's own 2026-09-07 Brand Consistency Review as a Minor, non-blocking fit-check (the moment being described there is an *active* functional onboarding — gaining real capability to open Sessions and sell — not a passive listing, and the verb alone doesn't fully carry that; the screen's own second line resolves the ambiguity in practice).
- **Never imply a merchant needed rescuing.** No "finalmente puedes..." or "ya no tienes que preocuparte por..." framing that casts her prior methods as a problem Nahui solved — she already knew what she was doing.
- **Technical terms never appear in merchant- or customer-facing copy** — `Session`, `SaleItem`, `Customer`, `Claim`, `subscriptionTier`, `registrationMode` and every other domain term stay in the code and the Foundation, never on screen. Restated here from `product/00-foundation/architecture-principles.md` #4 because it's as much a brand rule as a technical one — a leaked internal term breaks the companion illusion instantly. This now also covers `User`, `BusinessMembership`, `Invitation`, `role`, `OWNER`, `SELLER` — the new terms introduced by the Owner↔Seller relationship surface (below) — none of which may appear literally on any merchant- or customer-facing screen either.
- **Errors are stated plainly, without over-explaining or self-flagellating.** "No pudimos guardar tu cambio. Intenta de nuevo" — short, honest, no anxious over-apologizing (see `character-bible.md`'s "How Nahui apologizes," currently Hypothesis).

## Admitting a broken promise — **Decision** (the rules) / **Hypothesis** (the reception), added 2026-10-08

**Why this section exists.** The "Errors are stated plainly" bullet above governs *transient friction* — a save that failed, a code that didn't read, nothing lost, retry immediate. It does not govern the other class of apology-worthy moment, named for the first time in `brand/character-bible.md` §"The first real apology-worthy moment": Nahui said something that stopped being true, or committed to something and didn't do it, and the thing damaged is trust rather than work. The first real instance was the Aviso de Privacidad's stale "no tenemos ningún servidor" claim, corrected 2026-10-08 after real merchants had registered against it. These are the checkable tests; the identity-level answers are in the character bible.

- **Accountability grammar, and it's greppable.** The admission has a subject, and the subject is **"nosotros."** The institutional/bank register's disguise in an admission is the agentless passive: *"el aviso no fue actualizado oportunamente," "se identificó una inconsistencia," "por un error en el proceso," "se detectó una imprecisión."* Every one of those describes a lapse with nobody in it. *"Prometimos... y tardamos"* has someone in it. Where the original commitment was itself written passively, characterizing it as a promise *we made* is the right direction, not an overstatement.
- **Nahui is never the one who broke the promise.** The product stays in the third person for factual claims about itself ("Nahui no tenía ningún servidor"); the people own every accountability verb. *"Nahui tardó"* is a Blocker-class defect, not a stylistic preference — it makes her own companion the thing that failed her.
- **No apology-first structure, restated for this class.** The fact, then the consequence, then the fix. Where the remedy is complete and the reader is already holding it, the remedy carries the admission and an added "lo sentimos" spends a quarter of a short paragraph on our own posture — failure mode 6 below. **Where the remedy is not yet available, is partial, or cannot undo what happened, this rule does not apply and the question is genuinely open** (`character-bible.md`, same section).
- **Mitigation before the admission is chronology; mitigation after it is self-justification.** *"Eso era cierto cuando lo escribimos, y dejó de serlo..."* is a timeline. The same clause placed after the admission, as the paragraph's last word, is an excuse. Order decides which one the reader gets.
- **The consequence outranks the contrition, structurally.** The stressed position in the sentence belongs to what happened to real people, not to how late we were. A colon, or a following sentence, that puts the consequence after the admission does this; reversing them makes the paragraph about us.
- **The reader must not be able to read herself out of the affected set.** If people were affected and we don't know who, say that rather than describing a definite group of other people — a definite group is a claim one size larger than the evidence, in the comfortable direction (`brand-principles.md` principle 8).
- **Sized to the fact, in both directions.** The admission word matches the actual duration and severity. "Tardamos" is calibrated for weeks and is a minimization for a year. Understating a failure fails principle 8 exactly as inflating a capability does.
- **Characterizing our own prior words is held to the document's own accuracy standard.** Paraphrasing into merchant vocabulary is correct and preferred ("tu propio teléfono" for "tu propio navegador"). A paraphrase that broadens, softens, or strengthens what was actually promised is not — in the one passage whose job is to say what the document previously said, paraphrase is doing quotation's work.
- **Still no platform vocabulary, especially here.** What changed is named in her terms ("una base de datos en internet"), never *Supabase*, *backend*, *la nube*, *servidor de producción*. The technical-terms rule above does not relax because the subject is technical.
- **Out of register, explicitly:** *"lamentamos los inconvenientes"* and its whole family (institutional, and it apologizes for her inconvenience rather than for our inaccuracy); self-flagellation of any length; euphemism where the honest word exists (*"una inconsistencia," "un dato desactualizado"* where the fact is *"ya no era verdad"*); and any hedged minimizer (*"un pequeño retraso," "una imprecisión menor"*). Also out: making the admission a recurring motif. It is stated once, early, and the rest of the surface returns to plain present-tense description.

**Tier.** **Decision** for every rule above — deliberately adopted under `brand-guardian`'s ownership of `/brand/`, derived from already-adopted character-bible and tone-of-voice Decisions rather than introducing a new direction. **Hypothesis**, separately and deliberately, for the reception: **that a merchant reads a wordless, fix-first admission as plain rather than as cold is a claim about a reaction in someone else's head, and nobody has been asked.** The reasoning is strong (the remedy is complete, in hand, and in the same document; an apology on top would be the gesture substituting for the fix) and it remains untested. The rules bind regardless of how that Hypothesis resolves — the same split this document already applies to the investor register's persuasion claim. Among the cheapest things to test at the first real merchant contact.

## Speaking about Nahui to an investor or evaluator — **Decision**, added 2026-10-08

**What was answered, by whom, and what this section is.** The Product Owner answered the fourth-audience question on 2026-10-08, in the approved product definition for Nahui's public presentation page (`landing/BRIEF.md` §2): the audience is **investors and evaluators, in both Spanish and English** — people looking at Nahui from outside and judging whether it is a real company solving a real problem. The immediate evaluator is the professor of her ISOM5240 course, who fits the same profile: evaluates, does not buy. **The merchant is explicitly not a reader of that page.** The audience is her Decision and is not reopened here. The register that follows from it is a **Decision** taken under `brand-guardian`'s ownership of `/brand/`.

**One register, not one page.** This governs any surface whose reader is evaluating Nahui rather than using it: the presentation page, a future investor update, the spoken narration of a deck, a press note, an accelerator or grant application, an academic submission. Written down and dated here so the next such surface doesn't invent its own voice from scratch, which is how a company ends up with two personalities.

### The tension, taken seriously rather than waved at

Nahui's character is built on restraint. `character-bible.md` (Decision) says Nahui is "more comfortable in the background than the foreground," that it "never makes its own presence the center of a moment that belongs to her," and that it "never speaks from authority it hasn't earned." An investor page is a surface whose entire job is to put Nahui in the foreground and make it the subject of every sentence. That is a real tension, and it resolves in three moves rather than by exception.

**1. Nahui's humility is epistemic, not a vow of silence.** Read the humility rules closely and every one of them is about *standing relative to someone else's business*: don't instruct her, don't judge her choices, don't claim to know her trade, don't assume a view of her situation Nahui hasn't earned. Not one of them is a rule against stating plainly what Nahui is and what it has built. Stating a checkable fact about your own work, without hedging, is not unearned authority. Unearned authority is confidence about *someone else's* business, or about outcomes nobody has observed yet. A good investor page is dense with the first kind of claim and contains none of the second. That distinction, not volume, is what keeps this surface in character.

**2. The character doesn't pitch; the company speaks about the character.** Nahui-the-companion is Ana's. It should not be the voice asking anyone for money — borrowing her companion's intimacy to persuade a third party spends her relationship on someone else's decision. So on this surface Nahui is described, not performed: no first-person Nahui ("Hola, soy Nahui..."), no companion warmth aimed at the reader. The people who built it speak about what they built.

**3. The merchant stays the protagonist even where she is not the reader.** `storytelling.md`'s "the story is always Ana's" (Hypothesis) and principle 4, "the merchant is already an expert" (Decision), do not relax because she has left the room. They get *harder* here, because third-person description of a whole population is exactly where the rescue narrative enters a document unnoticed, and because the market-size framing an investor page reaches for is built out of describing people as a gap.

### How Nahui names itself — person, subject, and who's accountable

- **Third person for the product and what it does:** "Nahui registra ventas...," "Nahui está en piloto." / "Nahui records sales...," "Nahui is in pilot."
- **First person plural for the people who built it and for the limits of what they know:** "lo construimos," "aprendimos," "todavía no lo sabemos." / "we built," "we learned," "we don't know yet." Claims about the product are checkable facts about an artifact; learning and uncertainty belong to people, and an investor is in part evaluating the people. Writing "Nahui doesn't know yet" also drifts the companion character onto this surface, which move 2 above rules out.
- **"We" is for authorship and for uncertainty, never for destiny.** "Estamos transformando el comercio," "we're on a mission to..." is the pitch register (below), not this one.
- **Nahui never speaks in the first person here**, and the reader is never addressed in the companion register. See the second-person rule under "Spanish and English," below — it is the single most likely leftover from the archived page.
- **Whether the Product Owner is named is her call, not a brand call** — it is an identity and privacy question about a real person, and this document does not decide it. Register guidance either way: if she is named, the content is factual (what she built, what she decided, why), never a founder myth or an origin arc.

### How Nahui names its merchants in the third person

The largest brand risk on this surface, and the one most likely to read as a different company.

- **Her own word for what she does**, in both languages: she sells at bazares. Not "micro-entrepreneur," "informal merchant," "mom-and-pop seller," "SMB," "beneficiary," "usuaria/user" as her identity. Name the work, not a category invented to describe her from outside. (Which gendered form the Spanish uses stays **open** — see above; the page picks one and it is not a finding.)
- **The subject-position test, and it's greppable.** She is the subject of her own verbs. "Ana registra cada venta" — not "Nahui le permite registrar cada venta." *Permitir, empoderar, habilitar, ayudar a* / *enable, empower, unlock, allow* all put Nahui in the subject slot and her in the object slot. That is `storytelling.md`'s "What Nahui's origin story is not" (Decision) with the verbs rearranged, and it survives translation into any language. If the sentence can be rewritten with her as the subject and loses nothing, it was in the wrong voice.
- **Never a deficit description.** No "informal economy," "underserved," "unbanked," "sin acceso a," "rezago digital," "no sabe usar tecnología," "sin herramientas." A stated tool gap is allowed where it is a fact she herself reported and it describes a *tool*, not her: "no tenía cómo registrar cada venta sin perder al siguiente cliente" is a fact about registration; "le faltaba tecnología" is a judgment about her.
- **Count honesty on every population word.** A plural noun implies a number, and the number has to be one we have. "Negocios de vendedoras reales en producción" and a pilot of three are both true and both specific; "vendedoras en todo México" is not. `landing/BRIEF.md` §4 is the authoritative list of what may be claimed.
- **Privacy governs, and it is already decided** (`landing/BRIEF.md` §5; Product Owner, 2026-08-10): no description precise enough to identify the pilot merchant (exact assortment plus her zone identifies her in her own community, name or no name), and no words attributed to her that she did not say or approve.
- **The strongest available move is unavailable, and that's worth knowing before writing.** A real quoted merchant voice would be the single best execution of "the story is always Ana's" — and `storytelling.md`'s open question (obtaining Ana's own words with her consent) is **still open**. Until she gives them, paraphrase-and-attribute is the ceiling, per that document's own precedent. Nothing authorizes inventing warmth here to fill the gap.

### How progress gets stated without inflating it

- **Reuse the roadmap-honesty pattern, it was built for exactly this** (`storytelling.md`, Decision): past tense for what was built, present tense only for what runs today, an explicit label on anything that doesn't exist yet, in plain language rather than platform language ("En camino," not "Roadmap," "Beta," "Q3"). **Nahui never describes a capability it doesn't have in the tense of one it does** — this is the rule the pitch register breaks first.
- **Numbers rule.** A number appears only if it is countable from the repository, and it appears alongside what it counts. Two separate DMs asking for a photo per item is two, and the fact that it changed the domain model (D54) is the interesting part. No percentages, no latency, no "en segundos" / "in seconds" — `landing/BRIEF.md` §4 records that not one latency measurement exists in the whole repository and that `backlog.md` #1's own bar is unmet. No revenue, traction, or growth figures: there are none.
- **Specificity carries this page; adjectives don't.** Where an adjective is doing work a fact should do ("potente," "innovador," "intuitivo," "seamless," "powerful"), delete the adjective and state the fact. The true facts are unusually strong already (`landing/BRIEF.md` §4) and they are strong *because* they're checkable.
- **How Nahui is built is a fact, not a buzzword.** That Nahui is built by AI agents under auditable governance — a decision log, RFCs, specialist review, a project log — is one of its strongest true claims, and it earns a plain description. "AI-powered" as a decorative adjective is the same claim with the evidence removed.
- **Honest difficulty is allowed to be brief.** "En piloto, aprendiendo" is true and sufficient. "Los primeros resultados son prometedores" is a claim about other people's reactions that nobody has made; promising adoption would be a claim about the future on the one question that is genuinely open.
- **Hypothesis-tier claims stay off this surface — an extension of an existing bound, and a Decision.** `visual-language.md` bounded the untested perceptual readings of the mark ("a compass," "a person with open arms," "memorable") as not stateable *merchant- or customer-facing*. That bound now covers **any external surface, including this one.** An evaluator is precisely the reader who will discount an entire page over one unverifiable claim, and the readings are no more tested for her than for a merchant. Decision-tier meanings (the name's adopted meaning, the four pillars as what Nahui stands for) remain sayable as what the company chose.

### The failure modes, named explicitly

Four are already forbidden by `character-bible.md` and need restating in third person, because that's where they hide. One is specific to this surface. One is the over-correction the first five make tempting.

1. **Expert.** Explaining the Mexican bazaar economy to the reader from above, in the voice of a sector analyst. What Nahui actually has is one real interview, a small pilot, and one merchant's business understood well. Market framing sized larger than that fails "never claims certainty it doesn't have" (Decision) on the one surface where it is most tempting, and an evaluator can check it.
2. **Narrator.** A founding myth, a three-act arc, scene-setting about a merchant's day. The archived About line — "Nahui nació platicando con una vendedora... Ella conoce su negocio de memoria" — is in register and is the precedent (`storytelling.md`). A narrated interior life for a real woman who hasn't consented to it is not.
3. **Bank / fintech.** "Inclusión financiera," "no bancarizados," "rails," "payment infrastructure," "SMB enablement," plus the institutional-compliance cadence that comes with them. Nahui is not a fintech (`landing/BRIEF.md` §2) and payments are an explicit non-goal (`company/CLAUDE.md`) — so this register also smuggles in a product claim that isn't true.
4. **Virtual assistant.** "Nahui es tu asistente de IA para tu negocio." Contradicts `brand-principles.md` principle 1 (Decision) on the surface most likely to reach for the nearest familiar category, and principle 1 explicitly governs "what Nahui is and isn't allowed to say about itself, everywhere it appears."
5. **The generic startup pitch — the default gravity of every investor page ever written, and the one failure mode unique to this surface.** Its tells, listed so a reviewer can actually find them: category verbs and superlatives ("revolucionar," "transformar," "disrupt," "democratize," "the leading," "the first"); adjectives standing in for facts; market-size-as-argument ("un mercado de X mil millones"); manufactured momentum ("creciendo rápido," "traction," "momentum," "poised to"); the vision slide's present tense for things that don't exist; round numbers with no source; the destiny "we"; and vocabulary imported wholesale from decks ("leverage," "solution," "ecosystem," "at scale"). This register is not banned because it's distasteful. It's banned because nearly every one of its moves is a claim Nahui cannot substantiate, on a page whose reader's entire job is to check — and because `character-bible.md`'s "earned trust over assumed trust" (Decision) is the same rule read from the other end.
6. **Performed humility — the over-correction.** Self-deprecation ("apenas un proyectito," "todavía no tenemos nada"), apologizing for being early, hedging every sentence into mush. This fails for the same structural reason the pitch register does: it makes Nahui's own posture the subject instead of the facts, and it misstates the facts — there is a working product with real merchants' businesses in production. **Honest is not modest.** The discipline is *calibration*: each claim sized exactly to its evidence, neither larger nor smaller. "Hoy estamos en piloto" is calibrated. "Somos los primeros en..." and "no somos nada todavía" are both miscalibrated, in opposite directions, and the second is as off-brand as the first.

### Spanish and English: the same character twice, not a translation

- **Both versions complete and able to stand alone** (`landing/BRIEF.md` §3), each drafted in its own language from the same list of claims. Neither is a rendering of the other. The English is the only version the immediate evaluator will read, so nothing may be thinner there.
- **`global-principles.md`'s "never translate the UI" is untouched and not reopened.** It governs the merchant-facing product. This is a page that presents the company; different surface, per the brief's own §3.
- **The test that catches the defect that actually matters: the claims, their order, their tags, and their strength must be identical across languages — only the phrasing differs.** If one language hedges where the other asserts, or labels something as future where the other implies it exists, one of the two readers is being told something untrue. That is the most serious defect this page can ship, and it is checkable line by line.
- **Each language has its own failure direction.** The Spanish must read as natural Mexican business Spanish, not as translated English deck syntax — the real risk, since the claims will mostly be reasoned about in English. The English must read as plain English, not as literal Spanish word order. Same character, idiomatic twice.
- **Words that stay in Spanish inside the English version:** *Nahui*; *bazar / bazares* (gloss it once for a reader who may not know it — the recurring private pop-up markets where she sells — rather than swapping in a word that means something else to a non-Mexican reader); and any in-app label quoted from the product, since the hero phone stays in Spanish in both versions (`landing/BRIEF.md` §6). Where the English names something on screen, keep her word and gloss it ("Resultados, her results view"). Translating her screen into an English caption would contradict "never translate the UI" through the back door.
- **No second-person-possessive leak. This one is a Blocker wherever it appears.** Any "tu negocio / tus ventas / tu inventario / registras / ves cómo le fue" — or English "your business" — is addressing a reader who is not on this page. It is the archived page's merchant register surviving a copy-paste, and it is the exact failure the brief exists to prevent. It is also trivially greppable, so there is no excuse for shipping it.
- **The Spanish does not address this reader with the companion "tú" at all.** Prefer impersonal or infinitive constructions, including in CTAs ("Ver la app," "Conocer Nahui") over an intimate imperative. The CTA carve-out above still holds — naming a function the visitor is choosing is fine; borrowing the merchant's familiar register to do it is what's out.

### Tier, stated against this document's own discipline

**Decision** for the register — deliberately adopted, derived from existing `character-bible.md` Decisions plus the Product Owner's audience Decision and her own instruction: *"es orientada a inversionistas pero recuerda que somos honestos"* (2026-10-08). **Zero evidence, from anyone.** No investor, no evaluator, no professor has ever read any Nahui surface, and no one in that audience has been asked how a single line of this reads. Nothing in this section is **Validated**, and it would be a plain violation of `brand/CLAUDE.md`'s discipline to present it as if it were.

One claim inside it is deliberately **not** tagged Decision, because this discipline would be decorative if it exempted its own favorite argument: **that an honest, calibrated register is *more persuasive* to an investor than a pitch register is a Hypothesis.** The reasoning behind it is strong — an investor at pilot stage doesn't expect traction and does expect not to be lied to, and one inflated claim gets discounted by discounting everything else on the page — but it remains a claim about a reaction in someone else's head, which is exactly the kind of claim `/brand/` may never assert as fact. **The rule binds regardless of how that Hypothesis resolves.** It is what the Product Owner adopted, and it follows from "earned trust over assumed trust"; honesty here is not conditional on converting better.
