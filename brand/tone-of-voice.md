# Tone of Voice

Concrete language guidance — what `brand/character-bible.md`'s Voice section (Decision) sounds like in actual copy. This is what `ux-designer` and `marketing` consult directly when drafting merchant- or customer-facing text; `brand/brand-principles.md` states the "why," this document states the "how it reads."

Every claim below is a **Decision** unless marked otherwise — a direct, deliberate translation of already-adopted character-bible principles into language rules, not a new creative direction awaiting testing.

## The register

Direct, warm, plain. Business language before technical language — this document sits alongside, and never overrides, `product/00-foundation/global-principles.md`'s existing structural rule ("business language before technical language," natural Mexican Spanish, never a literal translation from English for anything merchant-facing). What this document adds is the *feeling* of that language: warm without being cute, direct without being blunt, honest without being clinical.

## Learner before advisor — the register that governs every recommendation

Direct expression of `character-bible.md`'s "Earning the right to advise" (Decision): Nahui should never sound like an expert, a narrator, a bank, a fintech, or a virtual assistant. It arrives knowing nothing about a given merchant, and learns from her before it ever gives advice — only after it has actually earned an understanding of *her specific* business does recommendation-shaped language belong in its copy at all.

- **Observation before instruction.** *"He notado algo..."* ("I've noticed something...") is the shape recommendation copy should take once Nahui does have something worth saying — never *"Deberías hacer esto"* ("You should do this"). The first offers something she can weigh; the second assumes a standing to instruct her that hasn't been earned. This is the single clearest test to run any recommendation-shaped copy against.
- **No prescriptive language without a real, merchant-specific basis behind it.** Words like "deberías," "te recomendamos," "lo correcto es" only belong in copy once there's an actual observation of *this* merchant's own data or behavior underneath them — a pattern in her own sales, a choice she's already made. Absent that basis (a brand-new merchant, a feature with no history to draw on yet), default to plain, neutral statement of fact, or to no comment at all, rather than manufacturing advice-shaped language to fill the space.
- **No expert/narrator/bank/fintech/assistant register, regardless of surface.** This applies as much to a notification or a report as to conversational copy: a confident, explaining tone that reads as "here is what you should know" rather than "here is what I noticed" fails this rule even where no literal imperative verb appears.
- **Age is not the axis.** A voice does not have to sound old, formal, or "wise" to violate this — a young, casual, confident "explainer" tone can claim exactly the same unearned authority a stiff corporate one does. What's being tested is whether the copy sounds like it already knows, not how old it sounds.

## What "never gives orders" governs — and what it doesn't — **Decision**, added 2026-10-07

`character-bible.md`'s "never gives orders — no imperative 'you should,' no urgency it hasn't earned" (Decision) is about **standing**, not about **grammar**. It was being read too literally: taken as a ban on the imperative mood as such, it would flag every button on every surface Nahui has ever shipped, including the imperative action labels throughout Approved `product/02-ux/` copy ("Registrar venta," "Continuar," "Cerrar jornada de venta," "No, gracias") and every CTA on `landing/index.html`. That reading is wrong, and this section draws the line explicitly so the rule isn't misapplied on a future review pass.

**The line.** The rule governs **advice- and recommendation-shaped copy** — any sentence in which Nahui expresses a view about what the merchant should do with her own business. It does not govern **control labels, CTAs, section headings, and headlines**, which name a function the merchant is choosing to invoke.

**Why the distinction is real and not a convenience.** A button label is not Nahui speaking to her; it is the name of the thing *she* is about to do. "Registrar venta" on a button is the merchant's own intent, written down so she can tap it — the grammatical imperative there is a label, not an instruction, exactly the way a drawer is labeled rather than commanded. "Deberías registrar tus ventas más seguido" is Nahui forming an opinion about how she runs her business and handing it to her unasked. Those two sentences share a verb mood and share nothing else: one reduces work (`character-bible.md`'s "How Nahui helps"), the other claims a standing Nahui has not earned ("Earning the right to advise"). Collapsing them would make the rule unenforceable, and — worse — would push copy toward limp, hedged control labels ("Quizás quieras registrar una venta") that are both harder to use and *more* patronizing than the plain imperative, failing `global-principles.md`'s "the best interface is the one that stays out of the merchant's way" and Value #4 at the same time.

**In the carve-out — the imperative is correct and preferred:**
- Button and control labels naming an action the merchant initiates: "Registrar venta," "Continuar," "Agregar producto," "Cerrar jornada de venta."
- Decline/escape affordances, which must stay as plain and prominent as the primary action: "No, gracias."
- Navigation, tabs, section headings: "Inventario," "Resultados," "Eventos."
- Marketing CTAs and headlines naming what the product does or what the visitor can do next: "Empezar gratis," "Prueba la demo," "Registra cada venta en segundos."
- Instructional microcopy inside a task the merchant has already chosen to start — the step-by-step of a flow she opted into ("Acerca la tag al teléfono"), which is orientation inside her own chosen task, not unsolicited advice about her business.

**Not in the carve-out — the rule applies in full:**
- Anything expressing a view on how she should run, price, stock, or grow her business: "Deberías activar esto," "Te conviene surtir más," "Lo correcto es cerrar la jornada cada día."
- Anything implying she has missed a step or fallen behind: "No olvides...," "Aún no has...," "Recuerda que deberías..."
- Manufactured urgency of any kind, in any grammatical mood — the separate "never use urgency Nahui hasn't earned" rule below stands independently and is not softened by this carve-out. "¡Aprovecha hoy!" is a CTA in form and a violation in substance.
- A control label that smuggles in a judgment rather than naming a function: a button reading "Arreglar mi inventario" is not in the carve-out, because "arreglar" asserts something was wrong.

**The test, for a reviewer.** Ask: *if the merchant read this and ignored it, would Nahui have had an opinion about her that she just declined?* If yes, it's advice-shaped and the rule applies. If there was no opinion to decline — only a function she didn't invoke — it's a control label and the carve-out holds. A finding raised against an imperative that passes this test is not a Blocker and not a Major; it isn't a finding at all.

## Gendered register in Spanish copy — **open gap, not decided here**, added 2026-10-07

**The gap.** No `/brand/` document has ever addressed how Nahui handles grammatical gender in Spanish copy, and the question is unavoidable in Spanish in a way it simply isn't in English — the language forces a choice on almost every noun and adjective referring to a person. `product/00-foundation/global-principles.md`'s Product Language rules cover *which* language and *whose* vocabulary, not this. The gap went unnoticed for as long as it did because in-product copy mostly addresses the merchant directly in second person ("tu negocio," "ya quedaste registrada"), where the choice is either invisible or already settled by who's reading. The first surface to need an actual rule is `landing/index.html`, which speaks *about* an audience rather than *to* one person.

**What that surface currently does — observed, not endorsed.** Three different patterns coexist on the same page:
- **Inclusive masculine for the audience:** "Para vendedores de bazar" (`hero.eyebrow`), "cuando abramos a más vendedores" (`band.lead`), "La app para vendedores de bazar en México" (`og:description`).
- **Feminine for evidence and story:** "nuestra primera entrevista con una vendedora," "Nahui nació platicando con una vendedora...," "Ella conoce su negocio de memoria," "aprendiendo con vendedoras reales," "cada paso lo probamos con vendedoras reales."
- **An explicit doublet in one place:** "la app para vendedoras y vendedores de bazar" (`meta description`).

Read charitably this is close to defensible — the feminine is factually correct wherever it refers to Ana and to the real women actually being interviewed, and the inclusive masculine is the conventional Mexican Spanish default for an open audience. Read less charitably, a visitor who notices will see the page address men and evidence women, and the one doublet makes it clear the question was felt but never settled.

**Why `brand-guardian` is not deciding this.** A gender-register rule for all merchant-facing Spanish would govern every current and future surface, in-product and external, and carries real positioning weight (who Nahui is visibly for) alongside the voice weight. That makes it a **Product Decision**, not a brand-execution call delegated to this document — routed to the Product Owner per `decision-ownership-classification`. The options below are drafted to make her call easier, not to pre-empt it. **None of them is adopted. Until she decides, existing copy is not a violation of this document**, and no Brand Consistency Review may raise a finding against gendered register on the basis of this section.

**Options, drafted neutrally.**

1. **Feminine as the default register throughout.** "Para vendedoras de bazar." Reflects the actual composition of the bazaar segment, makes Ana visible as the person the product was built with, and is the strongest positioning statement of the four. Cost: grammatically excludes male bazaar vendors, who do exist in the segment, and reads as a deliberate statement — which is either the point or a liability depending on the Product Owner's intent.
2. **Inclusive masculine as the default register throughout** (the conventional Mexican Spanish default, and what most of the current page already does). Lowest-friction, invisible to most readers, matches how merchants themselves commonly speak. Cost: the exact asymmetry visible on the page today — a brand whose entire evidence base and pilot user are women addressing its audience in the masculine; silently forgoes a positioning advantage that is factually earned.
3. **Doublets where the audience is plural or indefinite** ("vendedoras y vendedores"), natural gender where a real person is referred to. Explicitly inclusive, already present once on the page, defensible without being a statement. Cost: longer, heavier in headlines and tight UI space, and in merchant-facing Spanish it can read slightly institutional — brushing against this document's own "never corporate" register rule, which is a genuine brand concern and worth weighing as one.
4. **Avoid the construction where possible, natural gender where unavoidable.** Rewrite around the noun — "Para quien vende en bazares," "Para tu negocio de bazar" — rather than choosing a gender at all. Often produces the warmest and most merchant-vocabulary-native line of the four, and sidesteps the issue entirely in most headline positions. Cost: not always available, so it needs one of options 1-3 as the fallback rule anyway; and overused it can read evasive.

**What holds regardless of which option is chosen** — these are not open, and follow directly from existing Decisions:
- Where copy refers to a **real, specific person** (Ana, a real interviewee, a named merchant), natural gender is simply correct. "Una vendedora" describing a real woman is accurate, not a register choice, and no option above changes it.
- Whatever is chosen gets applied **consistently across in-product and external surfaces at once** — the whole purpose of this document is that the acquisition voice and the in-product voice are the same Nahui. A rule adopted for the landing page and not for the app would itself be a brand inconsistency.
- No option licenses `@`/`x` orthography ("vendedor@s," "vendedorxs") in merchant-facing copy. Out of scope of the four options above and inconsistent with `global-principles.md`'s "write how a Mexican bazaar vendor actually talks."
- Whatever is chosen, it is a **Decision**, not Validated — no merchant has been asked how any of these registers reads to her. Testable cheaply at the first real merchant contact.

## Concrete rules

- **State facts before offering an opinion.** "No pudimos guardar. Sigue aquí, intenta de nuevo" — what happened, then what to do — never an apology-first, fact-second structure.
- **Never use urgency Nahui hasn't earned.** No "¡Solo por hoy!," no manufactured scarcity, no countdown language — this is a companion, not a sales funnel. Directly enforces `character-bible.md`'s "never gives orders." *Note (2026-10-07):* this rule is not narrowed by the CTA carve-out above — manufactured urgency fails regardless of whether it appears in a button, a headline, or a sentence.
- **Suggestions read as offers, not instructions.** "Puedes revisar esto en Configuración" (an actual `reports.md` §3.13 precedent), never "Deberías activar esto" or any imperative construction implying she's missed a step. *Scope clarified 2026-10-07:* "any imperative construction" here means within advice-shaped copy — see "What 'never gives orders' governs," above, for why a control label naming a function she chose is not what this rule is about.
- **Celebration is about her, plainly stated, never inflated.** A completed sale, a reached milestone — state what happened in her terms ("Ya quedaste registrada con Ropa Ana"), never dress it up with exclamation-heavy congratulatory copy that centers the platform's cleverness. *Worth naming precisely, since it's since been reused elsewhere:* this exact sentence originates in `product/02-ux-loyalty/customer-loyalty-registration.md` — a **customer's** confirmation of joining a business's loyalty relationship, a passive, low-stakes "you're now recognized" moment. The sentence *shape* (plain, unadorned, her-achievement-first) generalizes cleanly to other registration/milestone moments; the specific verb ("quedaste registrada," you got registered/listed) carries a passive connotation worth double-checking on reuse — see `authentication.md` §3.10c's SELLER-invitation-acceptance reuse of this exact line, flagged in this document's own 2026-09-07 Brand Consistency Review as a Minor, non-blocking fit-check (the moment being described there is an *active* functional onboarding — gaining real capability to open Sessions and sell — not a passive listing, and the verb alone doesn't fully carry that; the screen's own second line resolves the ambiguity in practice).
- **Never imply a merchant needed rescuing.** No "finalmente puedes..." or "ya no tienes que preocuparte por..." framing that casts her prior methods as a problem Nahui solved — she already knew what she was doing.
- **Technical terms never appear in merchant- or customer-facing copy** — `Session`, `SaleItem`, `Customer`, `Claim`, `subscriptionTier`, `registrationMode` and every other domain term stay in the code and the Foundation, never on screen. Restated here from `product/00-foundation/architecture-principles.md` #4 because it's as much a brand rule as a technical one — a leaked internal term breaks the companion illusion instantly. This now also covers `User`, `BusinessMembership`, `Invitation`, `role`, `OWNER`, `SELLER` — the new terms introduced by the Owner↔Seller relationship surface (below) — none of which may appear literally on any merchant- or customer-facing screen either.
- **Errors are stated plainly, without over-explaining or self-flagellating.** "No pudimos guardar tu cambio. Intenta de nuevo" — short, honest, no anxious over-apologizing (see `character-bible.md`'s "How Nahui apologizes," currently Hypothesis).

## Three audiences, three contexts, one voice

Nahui speaks to three different people today — merchants and invited staff (both inside the Merchant Application) and customers (on the Loyalty-claim registration surface, `product/02-ux-loyalty/customer-loyalty-registration.md`) — and all three should still sound like the same underlying character, even though the relationship and stakes differ. **Added 2026-09-07:** this section previously named only two audiences; the third (a SELLER, invited staff who isn't the Business owner) is new as of `product-decisions.md` Q24/Q25's multi-staff surface, and is addressed here for the first time following a `brand-guardian` Brand Consistency Review of `authentication.md`, `home.md`, and `settings.md`'s new Owner↔Seller sections.

- **To the merchant (the Business owner)**: a companion who already trusts her expertise, and who is honest about how much of *her* business it has actually learned so far — see "Learner before advisor," above. Plain, warm, business-first.
- **To a SELLER (invited staff, not the Business owner)**: the same plain, respectful, non-judgmental, business-first voice as with the merchant — every existing rule in this document applies to her exactly as it applies to Ana, with no separate, softer, or more casual register invented for her. What's genuinely different, and deliberately so for now: today's SELLER-facing copy stays functional and factual — what she can do, what changed, an honest way to decline an invitation or accept one — rather than reaching for the merchant's own celebratory or advisory register. This isn't an oversight; it's the correct default while `character-bible.md`'s new "Relationship with a SELLER" section (Hypothesis) stays genuinely open on whether Nahui's relationship with a SELLER is the same companion relationship it has with Ana, or a narrower one. Revisit this bullet the moment a future feature asks Nahui to be warmer or more personally invested toward a SELLER specifically.
- **To the customer**: honest and unhurried, with an equally honest and equally easy way to decline — the existing `product/02-ux-loyalty/customer-loyalty-registration.md` spec's own "No, gracias" escape hatch, carrying equal prominence and availability to "Continuar," is the concrete example this document points to. No dark patterns, ever, in either direction.

**A fourth audience may exist and is not yet defined (added 2026-10-07).** `landing/index.html` ships an English-language string table alongside the Spanish one, and `company/brand/brand-guide.md`'s tagline, brand attributes, and brand promise are all written in English. Who that English reader is — an investor, an academic reviewer, a non-Mexican partner, a bilingual merchant — is an **open question routed to the Product Owner**, not answered here, and this document deliberately states no register for that reader until it is. `global-principles.md`'s "never translate the UI" rule is unaffected and unchallenged: it governs the merchant-facing product, and nothing in this note reopens it.

## What "Hypothesis" looks like here — worth naming explicitly

Some tone choices are still genuinely untested against real merchant/customer reaction:

- **How much warmth reads as "companion" vs. "assistant" in actual Spanish copy** — the character-bible's core distinction (Decision) hasn't yet been checked against whether merchants *feel* that distinction reading real screens. **Hypothesis.**
- **The exact register for apology/error copy** (`character-bible.md`'s own "How Nahui apologizes" section) — the existing error-state copy across `product/02-ux/` is a reasonable starting precedent, not yet confirmed as the right emotional pitch. **Hypothesis.**
- **Whether the merchant's own companion register extends to a SELLER at all, or stays functional-only** (`character-bible.md`'s new "Relationship with a SELLER" section) — added 2026-09-07, genuinely untested, and not yet needed to be resolved since no SELLER-facing copy today asks the question in practice. **Hypothesis.**

Note: "Learner before advisor," above, is not on this list. It is a Decision, not a Hypothesis — the Product Owner articulated it as a firm, present-day rule for how recommendation-shaped copy gets written now, not a direction awaiting merchant reaction to confirm. Neither is "What 'never gives orders' governs" (added 2026-10-07): it is a Decision too, and specifically a scope clarification of an existing Decision rather than a new claim — it narrows how an already-adopted rule is applied, it doesn't propose a new direction awaiting testing. "Gendered register in Spanish copy," also added 2026-10-07, is on neither list by design: it is an **open gap pending a Product Owner call**, carrying no tier at all until she makes one, because tagging an undecided question as Hypothesis would wrongly imply a direction had been chosen.

## How this gets used

- `ux-designer` and `marketing` consult this document directly when writing new copy, per the consultation triggers in `.claude/agents/brand-guardian.md`.
- `brand-guardian` reviews copy against these concrete rules during a Brand Consistency Review — a Blocker here means copy actively contradicts a stated rule (e.g., a leaked technical term, manufactured urgency, advice-shaped language with no merchant-specific basis behind it), not a matter of taste.
- **Two things in this document are explicitly not findable as findings:** an imperative that passes the control-label test in "What 'never gives orders' governs," and any gendered-register choice, while that question stays open. Raising either as a Blocker or Major would be a misapplication of this document, not an enforcement of it.
