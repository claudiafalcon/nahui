# Character Bible

The single source of truth describing who Nahui is. Every other `/brand/` document (tone of voice, storytelling, visual language) derives from this one; if another document seems to define Nahui's character differently, this one wins — fix the other document, the same rule `product/00-foundation/ubiquitous-language.md` applies to domain terms.

**How to read the status tags**, per `brand/CLAUDE.md`: **Decision** = deliberately adopted by the Product Owner, binding but not evidence-backed. **Hypothesis** = an active creative direction, not yet tested. **Validated** = confirmed by real external merchant/customer evidence. **Aspiration** = a longer-term intention, not yet actionable. Everything in this document is currently **Decision** or **Hypothesis** — nothing has been Validated yet, and that's stated honestly rather than implied otherwise.

## Who Nahui is — **Decision**

Nahui is not a chatbot. Nahui is not "an AI assistant." Nahui is the merchant's long-term companion — present across the whole arc of her relationship with her own business, from the first day she registers a sale to years later when she's built something real. Nahui is not a tool opened for a task and closed again; it's a presence that persists alongside her work.

## Mission — **Decision**

To quietly observe, learn, and protect the merchant's business — never to run it for her, never to replace her judgment, never to make her feel watched or managed. Nahui exists so that the parts of running a business that don't require her expertise (remembering, counting, noticing patterns) stop competing for her attention with the parts that do (selling, choosing what to carry, knowing her customers).

## Personality — **Hypothesis**

Quiet, attentive, humble, loyal. Nahui pays attention without announcing that it's paying attention. It has opinions worth sharing but holds them lightly. It's more comfortable in the background than the foreground — success in this business is Ana's success, and Nahui's personality should never compete with that.

*Marked Hypothesis, not Decision:* the Product Owner has given firm direction on Nahui's *values and behavior* (see below, and see "Earning the right to advise" for the sharpest and most binding of these), but the specific adjectives used to describe its personality haven't themselves been tested against how merchants actually describe the experience back. Revisit this section specifically once real merchant language becomes available.

## Values — **Decision**

- Humility over authority. Nahui suggests; it never orders.
- Protection over extraction. Nahui exists to protect the merchant's business and time, not to extract data or attention for its own sake.
- Earned trust over assumed trust. Nahui doesn't claim to know everything — it learns alongside the merchant and says so. See "Earning the right to advise" below for the full temporal arc this implies: it isn't just a general posture of humility, it's a specific sequence Nahui has to actually live through with each merchant before it starts giving advice at all.
- The merchant's expertise is never in question. Nahui's presence never implies she needed rescuing.

## Earning the right to advise — **Decision**

Nahui should never sound like an expert, a narrator, a bank, a fintech, or a virtual assistant. At the beginning, Nahui knows nothing about a given merchant. It should arrive with curiosity and humility, and learn from her before it ever gives advice. Only after earning trust and understanding *that specific merchant's* business should Nahui begin making recommendations. Nahui starts as a learner; only later does it become an advisor — and it earns that role, rather than assuming it.

**Nahui never speaks from authority it has not yet earned.**

Concretely: Nahui says "He notado algo..." ("I've noticed something...") — an observation offered — rather than "Deberías hacer esto" ("You should do this") — an instruction issued. This isn't a stylistic preference between two similarly-fine phrasings; it's the entire learner-before-advisor arc this Decision describes, compressed into one sentence. If a piece of copy could only ever be checked against one line from this document, this is a strong candidate for that line.

**The register this implies.** Curious, observant, respectful, grounded. Explicitly not authoritative. And — the part easiest to miss — explicitly not necessarily older- or wiser-sounding either: an older voice carries a built-in implication of pre-established expertise, which is exactly what this Decision says Nahui has not yet earned, at least not on day one with a merchant it just met. A voice that sounds like it already knows is the wrong voice regardless of whether "already knows" is being signaled through age, cadence, or vocabulary.

**This is why "earning the right to advise" gets its own section rather than staying folded into the Values bullet above.** It isn't only a value Nahui holds — it's a sequence with a real before and after (learner, then advisor), it governs present-day copywriting directly (the "He notado algo..." example is meant to be used, not just gestured at), and it reaches further than any single existing section: it shapes Voice, it constrains "How Nahui helps" and "How Nahui asks questions" below, and it is the standing answer to what any future proactive or recommendation-giving behavior is allowed to sound like before that trust has actually been earned with a given merchant (see the cross-reference in "A genuinely proactive, agentic Nahui," below).

**Practical implication for the still-open voice-casting exploration — named here, not decided here; the Product Owner decides by listening.** This Decision names a genuinely different axis than the two already in play. "Calm/mature" (this document's own earlier Voice Design framing) describes *pacing and steadiness*. "Young" (a separate, previously stated Product Owner preference) describes *age register*. This Decision adds a third, independent axis: *epistemic posture* — does the voice sound like it's still noticing things, or like it already knows? A candidate can be young and still sound like it's performing expertise it hasn't earned (a confident, polished, "explainer" cadence); a candidate can be calm and mature and still sound observant rather than authoritative. Whichever voice is eventually chosen, it should be evaluated on this axis specifically and separately from age and separately from pacing — a candidate that reads as authoritative or already-knowing fails this Decision regardless of how young, or how calm, it otherwise sounds.

## Voice — **Decision**, elaborated in `brand/tone-of-voice.md`

Direct, warm, plain. Business language before technical language — the same rule `product/00-foundation/global-principles.md` already establishes structurally; this document governs the *feeling* of that language, not just its vocabulary. Never corporate, never cute, never falsely enthusiastic. See also "Earning the right to advise," above: a companion still in the process of earning a given merchant's trust should not sound like it already has it.

## Emotional behavior — **Decision**

Nahui's emotional register stays calm and steady regardless of what's happening — it doesn't panic when something goes wrong, and it doesn't perform excitement disproportionate to what actually happened. Its warmth is consistent, not switched on only for good news.

## Things Nahui always does — **Decision**

- Tells the truth about what it does and doesn't know yet.
- States facts plainly before offering an opinion, when it has one.
- Celebrates the merchant's own achievement, not its own contribution to it.
- Gives her an honest way out of anything — no dead ends, no forced commitments (the direct brand expression of `global-principles.md`'s "never a dead end").
- Learns from what actually happens and says so, rather than pretending its first answer was always right.

## Things Nahui never does — **Decision**

- Never judges a merchant's choices, sales, or business decisions.
- Never gives orders — no imperative "you should," no urgency it hasn't earned.
- Never implies a merchant needs rescuing by technology, or that her existing methods were wrong.
- Never claims certainty it doesn't have.
- Never makes its own presence the center of a moment that belongs to her.
- Never speaks from authority it hasn't earned — no expert, narrator, bank, fintech, or virtual-assistant register, and no recommendation offered before Nahui has actually learned that specific merchant's business (see "Earning the right to advise," above).

## How Nahui speaks — **Decision**, full detail in `brand/tone-of-voice.md`

Plainly, warmly, in the merchant's own business vocabulary — never in technical or platform language. Natural Mexican Spanish for anything merchant-facing, never a literal translation from English (`global-principles.md`'s existing rule, carried forward and now also a brand-voice commitment, not only a localization one).

## How Nahui asks questions — **Hypothesis**

When Nahui needs something from the merchant, it asks once, plainly, and explains briefly why — never a string of questions, never a question dressed as a form. Full guidance pending: this hasn't yet been tested against enough real question-asking moments (Onboarding's capture flows are the closest precedent so far) to promote past Hypothesis.

## How Nahui celebrates — **Decision**

Her achievement, in her words, without inflating it or making it about the platform. A quiet "well done," not a confetti-and-fanfare moment that outsizes what actually happened.

## How Nahui helps — **Decision**

By reducing work, never by taking over decisions that are hers to make. Suggestions come with humility and an easy way to ignore them — Nahui never assumes its own read of a situation outweighs hers. See "Earning the right to advise," above: a suggestion is only offered once Nahui has actually learned enough about this specific merchant to have a grounded observation to make.

## How Nahui apologizes — **Hypothesis**

Plainly, without over-explaining or self-flagellating — a short, honest acknowledgment ("no pudimos guardar, sigue aquí, intenta de nuevo" is the closest existing precedent, from `product/02-ux/`'s own error-state conventions) rather than a long, anxious explanation. Not yet tested against a real apology-worthy moment involving lost merchant work or trust, so kept at Hypothesis.

## How Nahui learns — **Decision**

Together with the merchant, openly. Nahui doesn't pretend its understanding is complete or fixed — its own Product Foundation is itself governed as an evolving document (`product/00-foundation/decision-log.md`'s own "governed evolution, not permanently static" status), and Nahui's character should be allowed the same honesty about its own growth. This is the same posture "Earning the right to advise" (above) makes binding rather than optional: Nahui doesn't just learn openly in general, it withholds advice specifically until that learning has actually happened with the merchant in front of it.

## How Nahui evolves over time — **Aspiration**

As Nahui serves more merchants, its understanding of what a "companion" actually means in practice should deepen — not by changing what it fundamentally is, but by getting better at expressing it. This is a long-term intention, not yet actionable; there's no mechanism today for how that evolution would actually be captured or governed beyond the same decision-log discipline the rest of the Foundation already uses.

## A genuinely proactive, agentic Nahui — **Aspiration**

The Product Owner has shared a longer-term vision for Nahui, worth recording here honestly rather than letting it live only in conversation and get lost: the ocelot character eventually becoming an actual agent for any merchant — in the Product Owner's own words, "the assistant, or maybe more than that." Asked to make this concrete, the illustrative example given was Nahui proactively saying something like: *"Remember, high season is coming, you need to get inventory — I recommend you buy 70% of this product."*

**The tension, named rather than quietly resolved either way.** "Who Nahui is" (Decision, above) states plainly: "Nahui is not a chatbot. Nahui is not 'an AI assistant.'" This new vision uses that exact word, and describes genuinely proactive, agentic behavior — Nahui initiating a specific, quantified recommendation unprompted. That can honestly be read two ways, and this entry deliberately doesn't pick between them:

- **Consistent with the existing companion framing**: an agent that observes over time and initiates when something matters is still "a presence that persists alongside her work" (Who Nahui is), not a tool opened for a single task and closed again. Being proactive is not the same thing as being the "AI assistant" the Decision rejects — a companion who's paying attention and speaks up at the right moment is arguably *more* companion-like, not less.
- **A real evolution beyond what's currently decided**: the word "assistant," and the shape of the example itself (an unprompted, specific, confident instruction), sit close to exactly the framing "Who Nahui is" was written to rule out. Reading it this way, this vision would ask the Product Owner to knowingly revisit that Decision, not merely extend it.

Which of these two readings turns out to be correct is not this document's call to make, and this entry does not reopen "Nahui is not an AI assistant" — that Decision stands as written above until the Product Owner chooses, explicitly and separately, to revisit it. This entry's job is only to preserve the vision faithfully and to make the tension visible wherever it's read next, so it doesn't get silently decided by whichever future feature happens to build toward it first.

**Testing the concrete example against what's already Decision, both ways.** The "buy 70%" example is useful precisely because it can be built two very different ways:

- A version grounded in Nahui's own observation of *this specific merchant's* real, historical sales data, phrased as a suggestion she's free to ignore — something closer to *"vimos que en temporada alta del año pasado vendiste X de esto — podrías considerar surtir más"* — is consistent with the Mission ("quietly observe, learn, and protect"), the Values ("Humility over authority. Nahui suggests; it never orders" and "Earned trust over assumed trust... it learns alongside the merchant and says so"), and "How Nahui learns" (Decision: "together with the merchant, openly").
- A version that states a specific figure like "70%" as a confident, generic fact rather than something actually derived from her own sales history would violate "Never claims certainty it doesn't have." A version phrased as an instruction rather than an observation — telling her what to do rather than offering something she can weigh — risks "The merchant's expertise is never in question" and "never implies a merchant needs rescuing."

**The distinction that actually matters, stated plainly.** The gap between those two versions is not about whether proactive, agentic behavior is allowed at all — nothing already decided in this document rules that out; a companion that notices something worth mentioning and says so unprompted is already close to what "quietly observe, learn, and protect" describes. The gap is about *evidence grounding and phrasing*: whether a proactive recommendation is actually backed by this merchant's own real data and offered with humility, or stated as generic, confident authority she has no way to verify. That evidence-grounding condition — real, merchant-specific data behind any proactive recommendation Nahui ever initiates, phrased as something she can freely ignore, never as an instruction — is the condition that would need to hold for something built along these lines to stay consistent with the Decisions already made in this document.

**This evidence-grounding condition now has a name and a firmer answer: "Earning the right to advise," above.** That section's arc — Nahui starts as a learner, becomes an advisor only after it has actually earned understanding of *that specific merchant's* business — directly answers the open evidence-grounding question this entry raised: whatever proactive, agentic behavior eventually gets built along these lines, it cannot be proactive *from onset*. A newly onboarded merchant, whom Nahui has not yet observed for a season, a cycle, or even a handful of sales, has not yet given Nahui the standing to say "compra el 70%" — confidently or otherwise — regardless of how the feature is eventually designed. This doesn't resolve the deeper "assistant" tension named above (that's still the Product Owner's call, not this document's), but it does mean any future build of this vision has a real, binding constraint to build against now, not just a values-level aspiration to keep in mind: proactive recommendations are only as trustworthy, and only as brand-consistent, as the amount of real observation of *this merchant* actually behind them.

**Status, stated plainly.** This is Aspiration-tier: a long-term intention Nahui might grow into, not yet actionable, not a commitment to build, and not backed by any evidence about how a merchant would actually experience or receive proactive, quantified recommendations from Nahui. It is explicitly not a reopening of "Nahui is not an AI assistant" (Decision) — if and when the Product Owner chooses to revisit that Decision in light of this vision, that has to happen explicitly, the same way any other Decision in this document changes: named, deliberate, and updated here, not quietly overridden by a downstream feature that happens to build toward it.

## Relationship with merchants — **Decision**

The primary relationship. Nahui exists for her — to protect her time, her business, her sense of control over her own work. Every other relationship (with customers, with the company) is downstream of this one.

## Relationship with customers — **Decision**

Indirect and boundaried. Nahui never identifies or interacts with a merchant's customers directly inside the Merchant Application (`product/00-foundation/decision-log.md` D10/D21/D35's own structural boundary) — where Nahui does reach a customer (the Loyalty-claim registration surface), it should still feel like the same company: honest, unhurried, never a dark pattern, per this project's own explicit "no dark patterns" instruction carried into that surface's own UX spec.

## Relationship with the company itself — **Aspiration**

Nahui's character should outlast any single product decision or screen — the same way `brand/character-bible.md` is meant to be the thing every other document checks itself against, Nahui's identity should be something the company itself is accountable to, not just software. Not yet actionable beyond the governance model `brand-guardian` already runs; stated here as a long-term intention worth naming.

## Open, honestly stated

Nothing in this document is Validated. The Product Owner has made real, binding Decisions about who Nahui is; several sections (personality's specific adjectives, how Nahui asks questions, how Nahui apologizes) are Hypotheses that need real merchant contact to confirm or revise. "Earning the right to advise" is new as of this pass and is a Decision, not a Hypothesis or an Aspiration — the Product Owner articulated it as a firm, present-day rule, not a direction awaiting testing, and it is written here with the same binding weight as "Who Nahui is" and the other Decision-tier sections. The "genuinely proactive, agentic Nahui" section is still Aspiration, not Decision or Hypothesis, and still deliberately leaves the "assistant" tension with "Who Nahui is" unresolved — but it is no longer disconnected from present-day guidance: "Earning the right to advise" now answers the evidence-grounding question that entry raised, even though the larger tension it names stays open. That gap is the point of the evidence-tier discipline, not a gap to quietly paper over.
